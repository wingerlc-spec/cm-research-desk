Research date: November 17, 2026. Exchange rates: sources' own conversions are quoted as given (they range from about 7.1 yuan per dollar for 2025 deals to about 6.7 for late-2026 items). The only official spot rate read this session is 6.7053 yuan per US dollar at end-September 2026 (PBoC Q3 report as relayed by People's Daily). No conversions of my own are made unless labelled.

## Chinese technology and AI funding: who is borrowing, how much, at what coupons

### Takeaway
Chinese tech majors have borrowed in offshore renminbi at coupons of roughly 1.9% to 3.1% (2025 deals) and about 2.6% on long-term yuan bonds in H1 2026, against about 4.4% average effective cost on US hyperscalers' long-term bonds, but bonds are a minor channel: Chinese AI funding leans on bank loans, equity placements and state-linked capital. The 2025 "technology board" bond programme produced about 2.3 trillion yuan of sci-tech bonds in 2025, of which 87% by value came from state-owned issuers and under 8% from private firms, with over half of proceeds used to repay or swap debt.

### Inferences
- The hypothesis holds at the level of headline coupons for top-rated Chinese platforms: they borrow in renminbi roughly 2 to 3 percentage points below US peers' dollar coupons. It holds less well as a description of how the build-out is financed: loans and equity dominate, 87% of the firms' outstanding bonds are legacy dollar paper, and the flagship sci-tech bond programme is mostly SOE and financial issuers refinancing debt.
- Alibaba's choice of a zero-coupon dollar convertible and an HK$80 billion share placement suggests equity-linked funding is at least as important as cheap renminbi debt for the largest private spender.

## State channels: sovereign special bonds, local special bonds, policy banks, PBoC relending, state funds

### Takeaway
The state channels are large relative to corporate tech borrowing: 1.3 trillion yuan of ultra-long special treasury bonds and a 4.4 trillion yuan local special bond quota in 2026, 800 billion yuan of policy-bank "policy-based financial instruments" (up from 500 billion yuan in 2025), and a PBoC technology relending quota raised to 1.4 trillion yuan. A meaningful share of these is directed at equipment upgrades, "six networks" infrastructure including computing power, and AI or digital projects, though part of local special bond proceeds is going to repay hidden debt and arrears.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Summing the three 2026 bond lines in the BOFIT note (1.3 + 4.4 + 0.3 trillion yuan) gives about 6.0 trillion yuan of special-purpose government bond quota, before the ordinary deficit. This is arithmetic on cited figures, not a sourced total.
- State-funded computing build-out is dominated by state telecom operators and policy-bank-backed projects, funded at relending or PSL rates of 1.25% to 1.5%, well below the 3.0% LPR. The cheapest renminbi funding therefore reaches state entities and designated projects first.

## Borrowing costs in China versus US AI and data center borrowers

### Takeaway
China's benchmark lending rate has been frozen at 3.0% (one-year LPR) and 3.5% (five-year) for 17 months through October 2026, the 10-year government bond yield fell below 1.7% in September 2026, and top Chinese tech names borrow at 2% to 3%. In the US, yields have risen sharply during 2026: Meta's November 2026 dollar bonds carried coupons from 5.85% (5-year) to 7.50% (30-year) and CoreWeave pays 9.75% on unsecured notes.

## Real cost of capital and whether low nominal rates are stimulative

### Takeaway
The premise that Chinese producer prices are falling is out of date: after almost four years of declines PPI turned positive in 2026, topped 4% in June, and was +5.7% year on year in October 2026, while CPI was +0.7%. Measured against PPI, real borrowing costs in China are now negative; measured against CPI they remain around 2%. The rise is concentrated in upstream energy and materials, consumer-goods factory prices are still slightly negative, and economists cited say rate sensitivity is low and low yields reflect weak demand and trapped savings.

### Cited Findings
- China PPI October 2026: +5.7% year on year, +0.7% month on month; January to October average +2.7%. Mining and quarrying +27.9%, raw materials +11.6%, processing +3.5%; consumer goods −0.1%. Coal mining +36.6%, oil and gas extraction +35.0%. Input purchase prices +7.9% — [National Bureau of Statistics, Nov 2026](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202611/t20261111_1965806.html)
- China CPI October 2026: +0.7% year on year, flat month on month; January to October average +0.9% — [CGTN, Nov 9 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-11-09/China-s-October-CPI-up-0-7-PPI-rises-5-7--1RVFvJks2Fa/p.html)
- September 2026: PPI +5.0%, CPI +0.9%. Drivers cited include energy and geopolitics, capacity management in solar, batteries and autos, and an AI-related rise in chip, computer and phone prices (Golden Credit Rating). Galaxy Securities: gains are concentrated upstream; if end demand stays limited and energy support fades, PPI is more likely to pull back — [Yicai Global](https://www.yicaiglobal.com/news/chinas-ppi-cpi-gap-widens-in-october-amid-rising-factory-prices-weak-demand-recovery)
- PPI was negative for almost four years before turning positive and topping 4% in June 2026; the GDP deflator turned positive in Q2 2026 after four years of negative readings. Barclays: "If one were looking only at the fiscal profile, China's bond yields should be higher than the West's, not lower." The column's argument is that low yields reflect "trapped money with nowhere else to go": savings at 43% of GDP, capital controls, a property crash since 2021. ### Inferences
(Simple subtraction on cited figures; the choice of deflator drives the answer.)
- China, one-year LPR 3.0%: minus October 2026 CPI of 0.7% gives about +2.3% real; minus October PPI of 5.7% gives about −2.7% real; minus January to October average PPI of 2.7% gives about +0.3%.
- China, Tencent/Baidu long-term yuan bonds at about 2.6%: about +1.9% against CPI, about −3.1% against October PPI.
- US, Meta 5-year at 5.85%: minus September 2026 CPI of 3.7% gives about +2.2% real; Meta 10-year at 6.55% gives about +2.9%.
- On a CPI basis the real cost for top-tier tech borrowers is therefore similar in the two countries (roughly 2%), and the nominal advantage is largely an inflation differential. On a PPI basis China's real cost is far lower in late 2026, a reversal from 2025 when falling PPI made Chinese real rates high. For AI and platform firms, whose output prices (cloud, tokens, consumer goods) are still falling, the PPI-based figure probably overstates relief, since the PPI rise is upstream energy and materials while consumer-goods PPI is −0.1%.
- The 2025 version of the argument (deflation makes Chinese real rates high) and the late-2026 version differ; a report should date any real-rate claim.

## Scale comparison: Chinese versus US AI capex and debt issuance

### Takeaway
Chinese AI capex is roughly one-sixth of US levels in 2026 (about US$140 billion versus US$750 to 800 billion), and US hyperscalers' bond issuance alone (US$132 to 194 billion in the first seven months of 2026, depending on the count) is of the same order as China's entire AI capex.

## Constraints on China's side and whether credit growth is accelerating

### Takeaway
Credit growth is not accelerating: outstanding yuan loan growth hit a record low of 4.7% in October 2026 with net loan repayments, and total social financing growth of 7.2% is being carried by government bonds. Chip access, thin cloud and model margins, LGFV deleveraging and a state-skewed bond market all limit how much cheap credit turns into productive tech investment.

### Cited Findings
- October 2026: new yuan loans contracted by 100 billion yuan (third monthly contraction of the year per the headline); January to October new loans 11.42 trillion yuan versus 14.97 trillion a year earlier; outstanding loan growth 4.7% (record low); TSF growth 7.2%; M2 7.1%; household loans −430 billion yuan and corporate loans −160 billion yuan in the month. ANZ's Zhaopeng Xing: medium- and long-term household and corporate loans turned to net repayments. Guotai Junan's Zhou Hao: "Aggregate financing still relies mainly on bond issuance" — [The Standard/Reuters, Nov 2026](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/348457/Chinas-October-new-bank-loans-shrink-for-third-time-this-year-as-credit-demand-weakens)
- January 2026: loan growth 6.1% (then a record low); 12-month new lending fell from above 24 trillion yuan in early 2023 to 15.6 trillion; SOE share of lending rising and private share falling; land sales revenue fell about 15% in 2025 to 4.15 trillion yuan (2021 peak 8.7 trillion) — [Rhodium, Feb 2026](https://rhg.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/RHG_China_FinancialFiscalDecay_19Feb2026.pdf)
- Local government debt: LGFV bond net financing negative in H1 2026 on and offshore; land sale revenue down 31.5%; non-standard defaults concentrated in Shaanxi, Shandong, Yunnan, Guizhou, Henan — [S&P Global China Ratings](https://www.spgchinaratings.cn/upload/20260818_LGFV%20Mid-Year%20Review-.pdf)
- Chips: as of May 14, 2026 no Nvidia H200 chips had shipped to China since US authorisation in December 2025; about 10 Chinese firms held US licences (up to 75,000 units each, 25% revenue share to the US government) but Beijing had paused orders pending a supply-chain security review; Nvidia guidance assumed no China revenue — [Implicator, May 2026](https://www.implicator.ai/nvidia-h200-deliveries-to-china-remain-stalled-after-trump-xi-summit/). Rhodium (September 2026) says chip shortages limit capex, SMIC supply is rising and H200 access is easing — [Rhodium](https://rhg.com/research/examining-chinas-ai-financing/). Moody's calls chip access China's biggest constraint and says China's cost advantages lie in non-chip infrastructure — [technode](https://technode.global/2026/08/28/chinese-hyperscalers-ramp-up-ai-spending-but-trail-us-rivals-moodys/)
- Profitability and price competition: AliCloud adjusted EBITA margin 9% (2025) and 10% (H1 2026) versus Amazon cloud 35% and 39%; Chinese AI model ARR about US$10.7 billion, roughly 10% of OpenAI plus Anthropic; Zhipu gross margin 26% and MiniMax 18% in H1 2026 (41% and 25% in 2025); Alibaba's AI app segment lost 14 billion yuan adjusted EBITA on 3 billion yuan revenue in Q2 2026; Alibaba e-commerce adjusted EBITA fell 44% in FY2026 — [Rhodium](https://rhg.com/research/examining-chinas-ai-financing/)
- Sci-tech bonds flow mostly to SOEs (87%) with private firms under 8%, attributed to rating models that penalise asset-light innovators — [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-04-03/in-depth-chinas-sci-tech-bond-boom-conceals-sidelined-private-sector-102430643.html)
- Utilisation: only 13.6% of intelligent computing projects were operating in January to September 2026 — [technode](https://technode.com/2026/11/11/china-accelerates-data-center-expansion-as-the-west-faces-mounting-opposition/)

### Inferences
- Weak private credit demand is the reason rates are low, so low rates are partly a symptom. Banks pricing loans below LPR for names such as Tencent is consistent with banks competing for the few strong private borrowers.
- The binding constraints on Chinese AI investment appear to be chips and returns, not the price of debt.

### Gaps
