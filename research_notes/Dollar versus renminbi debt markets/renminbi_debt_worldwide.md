## Onshore bond market: size, composition, issuance, deficit targets and augmented debt

### Takeaway
China's onshore bond market stood at CNY 210.8 trn at end-September 2026 (about USD 31 trn at 6.75), a little more than double the CNY 99.1 trn outstanding at end-2019, with growth driven by government borrowing (net government bond financing of CNY 13.8 trn in 2025). The IMF puts general government debt at 75.1% of GDP and augmented debt at 135.3% of GDP for 2026. The split of the stock by bond type was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Total bond market custody balance: CNY 210.8 trn at end-September 2026 (PBoC September 2026 financial market report). At 6.75 this is about USD 31.2 trn (my conversion) — [Xinhua Finance / cnfin](https://www.cnfin.com/zs-lb/detail/20261026/4486510_1.html); same figures in [Beijing Daily](https://news.bjd.com.cn/2026/10/26/12007848.shtml)
- Total custody balance: CNY 196.7 trn at end-2025, "about 28 trillion U.S. dollars" (source conversion) — [Yicai/Xinhua](https://www2.yicaiglobal.com/news/chinas-bond-market-custody-balance-hits-cny-1967-trln-at-end-2025)
- Total bonds outstanding: CNY 99.1 trn at end-December 2019 (about USD 14.4 trn at 6.86, my conversion). Gross issuance in 2019 was CNY 45.3 trn (about USD 6.6 trn, source conversion), up 3.1% on 2018: treasury bonds CNY 4 trn, local government bonds CNY 4.4 trn, financial bonds CNY 6.9 trn, corporate debentures CNY 9.7 trn (the listed categories sum to CNY 25 trn, so the article omits some types, probably interbank certificates of deposit) — [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202001/19/WS5e240b94a310128217272077.html)

- Net financing in September 2026 (single month): government bonds CNY 1,536.22 bn, up CNY 346.96 bn year on year; corporate bonds minus CNY 39.26 bn (net repayment), CNY 52.9 bn lower year on year — [Xinhua Finance / cnfin](https://www.cnfin.com/zs-lb/detail/20261026/4486510_1.html)
- ADB measure (different coverage): local currency debt securities outstanding CNY 183.5 trn at end-Q2 2026, up 2.4% quarter on quarter, of which bonds with maturity over one year CNY 173.5 trn; growth driven by corporate issuance — [AsianBondsOnline](https://asianbondsonline.adb.org/china/market-summary/)
- 2026 budget (Government Work Report, March 2026): deficit about 4% of GDP, CNY 5.89 trn, CNY 230 bn more than 2025; ultra-long special treasury bonds CNY 1.3 trn (unchanged from 2025); local government special bonds CNY 4.4 trn (unchanged); special treasury bonds to recapitalise large state banks CNY 300 bn (CNY 200 bn less than 2025). The flat quotas were below market expectations — [MNI](https://www.mnimarkets.com/articles/mni-brief-china-keeps-fiscal-expansion-similar-to-2025-npc-1772679088621)
- IMF 2025 Article IV (press release 18 Feb 2026), % of GDP: general government debt 60.9 (2024), 68.4 (2025), 75.1 (2026), 78.9 (2027), 86.9 (2030). Augmented debt 126.6 (2025), 135.3 (2026), 141.5 (2027), 153.7 (2030). Augmented net borrowing 14.3 (2025), 14.2 (2026), 13.7 (2027) — [IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/02/18/pr-26053-china-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation)
- Monetary backdrop: no actual easing recently despite an "appropriately accommodative" label; policy rates and reserve requirements last changed in spring 2025; the PBoC added overnight operations in June 2026 — [BOFIT Weekly 36/2026](https://www.bofit.fi/en/monitoring/weekly/2026/vw202636_1/)

### Inferences
- The stock grew by CNY 111.7 trn between end-2019 and end-September 2026 (210.8 minus 99.1), a factor of 2.13. From end-2025 to end-September 2026 it grew by CNY 14.1 trn, or 7.2% in nine months (my arithmetic).
- Adding the three 2026 quotas outside the headline deficit (1.3 + 4.4 + 0.3 = CNY 6.0 trn) to the CNY 5.89 trn deficit gives about CNY 11.9 trn of planned new government borrowing in 2026. This is my sum, not an official total, and it excludes any local debt-swap refinancing quota.
- In US dollar terms the 2019 to 2026 increase looks slightly larger than in renminbi terms because the conversion rate used moved from 6.86 to 6.75; the comparison is sensitive to the rate chosen.

## Who holds Chinese bonds: domestic versus foreign

### Takeaway
Foreign investors hold a very small slice: CNY 3.20 trn in the interbank market at end-June 2026, about 1.8% of custody, mostly central government bonds. Holdings fell for roughly a year from spring 2025 before a first monthly rise in May 2026; one source puts the June 2026 level about 28% (CNY 1.25 trn) below the peak.

### Cited Findings
- Overseas institutions' custody balance: CNY 3.5 trn at end-2025, 1.8% of the total; 56 new overseas institutions entered the interbank market in 2025 — [Yicai/Xinhua](https://www2.yicaiglobal.com/news/chinas-bond-market-custody-balance-hits-cny-1967-trln-at-end-2025)
- Interbank holdings: CNY 3.12 trn at end-April 2026 and CNY 3.21 trn (about USD 475 bn, source conversion) at end-May 2026; May was the first monthly increase since April 2025. Context given: China's market was "largely insulated" from a global sell-off in which US, UK, euro-area and Japanese yields rose 35 to 60 basis points after the outbreak of the Iran conflict — [Reuters via StratNews Global](https://stratnewsglobal.com/world-news/china-sees-first-foreign-bond-inflows-since-april-2025/)
- End-June 2026 (PBoC Shanghai head office): CNY 3.20 trn, about 1.8% of interbank custody. By type: government bonds CNY 2.01 trn (62.8%), policy bank bonds CNY 0.75 trn (23.4%), interbank certificates of deposit CNY 0.28 trn (8.8%), other CNY 0.16 trn (5.0%). 1,200 overseas institutions: 841 via Bond Connect, 635 via settlement agents (CIBM Direct), 276 using both. June cash bond trading by foreigners about CNY 1.71 trn, about CNY 81.4 bn a day — [Xinhua Finance / cnfin](https://www.cnfin.com/zs-lb/detail/20260717/4442346_1.html)
- Foreign holdings about CNY 3.2 trn at end-June 2026, "down roughly 28% (1.25 trillion RMB) from the peak, after eleven consecutive months of net selling through March"; low onshore yields make hedged returns unattractive — [IAI brief](https://www.iai.it/sites/default/files/iaibrief2644.pdf)
- 35 new overseas institutions entered the interbank market in January to September 2026 — [Xinhua Finance / cnfin](https://www.cnfin.com/zs-lb/detail/20261026/4486510_1.html)
- Foreign investment in Chinese bonds has been limited since 2021, attributed to low Chinese yields and geopolitical risk — [RBA Bulletin, Aug 2026](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2026/aug/developments-in-rmb-internationalisation.html)
- Bloomberg headline, 16 April 2026: "Foreign Investors Keep Selling Chinese Bonds Despite Resilience" (headline only; article not read) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-16/foreign-investors-keep-selling-chinese-bonds-despite-resilience)

### Inferences
- The IAI figures imply a peak of about CNY 4.45 trn (3.2 plus 1.25). The peak date and the exact peak value were not read in a primary source, so treat this as derived.
- From end-2025 (CNY 3.5 trn) to end-June 2026 (CNY 3.20 trn) holdings fell by about CNY 0.3 trn. The two figures may differ slightly in scope (whole market versus interbank only).
- Minor conflict on timing: Reuters says May 2026 was the first inflow since April 2025 (implying selling through April 2026); IAI says eleven months of net selling through March 2026.
- Roughly 98% of the market is held domestically, so foreign selling has little effect on funding. This follows from the 1.8% share.

- Foreign holdings for July to October 2026: not found (the October release page returned unreadable content).
- Split of foreign holdings by channel in value terms (Bond Connect versus CIBM Direct): not found, only institution counts.

## Offshore and cross-border renminbi debt: dim sum bonds, panda bonds, bank loans, loan conversions, swap lines

### Takeaway
This is where growth is fastest. Panda issuance reached CNY 245.05 bn in January to September 2026, already above the 2025 full-year total of CNY 183.06 bn; dim sum issuance set a record in 2025 and roughly doubled again in the first half of 2026; and Chinese banks' overseas lending in renminbi overtook their foreign-currency lending in early 2026. The main stated reason is cost: renminbi funding has been cheaper than dollar funding since 2022.

Swap lines
- The PBoC has signed over 40 bilateral swap lines, including one with the Reserve Bank of Australia — [RBA Bulletin, Aug 2026](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2026/aug/developments-in-rmb-internationalisation.html)

### Inferences
- Panda issuance in the first nine months of 2026 (CNY 245.05 bn, about USD 36 bn at 6.75) was 134% of the whole of 2025 (CNY 183.06 bn). On that pace 2026 is a record year by a wide margin.
- Who is borrowing: (1) European and US banks and multinationals funding China operations (Deutsche Bank, Barclays, BNP Paribas, Morgan Stanley, Volkswagen, Henkel); (2) emerging-market sovereigns and quasi-sovereigns with trade or debt links to China (Kazakhstan, Pakistan, Indonesia, Slovenia as a euro-area exception); (3) Chinese technology firms and state enterprises refinancing dollar debt offshore in renminbi. The shift in the offshore issuer mix from 50% to 80% offshore institutions suggests foreign borrowers, not only Chinese ones, are driving 2026 volumes.
- Why: a rate gap of roughly 1 to 2 percentage points on three-year money in 2025, a rising renminbi (cutting hedging cost concerns for investors but adding currency risk for unhedged borrowers), and for African sovereigns a way to obtain debt relief from Chinese lenders without a formal restructuring.
- The Kenyan-style conversions change the currency of existing debt and do not add to the stock, so the growth in renminbi lending overstates new credit.
- Scale check: dim sum stock of CNY 2.0 to 2.6 trn plus panda stock of over CNY 0.4 trn is roughly CNY 2.4 to 3.0 trn (about USD 360 to 440 bn at 6.75), around 1% to 1.5% of the CNY 210.8 trn onshore market. Reuters' figure of 0.2% for "international yuan debt" relative to the domestic market in December 2025 uses a narrower definition; the two are not comparable.

## The renminbi's share in global systems

### Takeaway
The renminbi's shares are small and uneven: about 2% of disclosed reserves (below its 2.8% peak in 2022), 2.38% of SWIFT payments (sixth), but 9.14% of trade finance (second) and roughly 30% to 35% of China's own trade settlement. Its position is strongest where China is itself a party to the transaction.

### Cited Findings
- COFER: renminbi 2.0% of reserves at Q4 2025, holdings USD 0.26 trn, a small rise after declining since Q1 2022; dollar 56.8% (USD 7.46 trn), euro USD 2.66 trn, yen USD 0.76 trn, sterling USD 0.58 trn — [Wolf Street](https://wolfstreet.com/?p=111125)
- COFER history: 1.1% in 2016, 2.8% in 2022, about or just below 2% by 2026 — [RBA Bulletin](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2026/aug/developments-in-rmb-internationalisation.html)
- Dollar share: 56.4% at Q4 2025 and 57.1% at Q1 2026 (56.8% at constant exchange rates) — [Econbrowser](https://econbrowser.com/?p=61297). Note the Q4 2025 dollar share differs between sources (56.4% versus 56.8%), probably because of data revisions or a different denominator.
- Renminbi about 2% of global reserves and sixth-ranked as of July 2026 — [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/econographics/the-imfs-boost-to-chinas-currency-is-costing-the-world-ten-years-later/); "2.1-2.3 per cent" in [IAI brief](https://www.iai.it/sites/default/files/iaibrief2644.pdf)
- SWIFT, August 2026: renminbi 2.38% of global payments by value, rank 6 (3.26% and rank 5 excluding intra-euro-area payments). Dollar 59.87%, euro 13.25%, yen 4.93%, sterling 4.83%, Canadian dollar 2.45%. Trade finance: renminbi 9.14%, rank 2; dollar 80.68%. Hong Kong handles 75.94% of offshore renminbi payments, the UK 7.06%, Singapore 3.96% (the lower-ranked labels were matched by order and should be checked) — [SWIFT Global Currency Tracker, Sep 2026](https://www.swift.com/sites/default/files/files/global-currency-tracker_september-2026.pdf)
- SWIFT, December 2025: 2.73% in the text of the City of London report, 2.78% in its chart (internally inconsistent) — [City of London report](https://www.theglobalcity.uk/PositiveWebsite/media/Research-reports/London-RMB_June-2026_Digital.pdf)
- China's own trade: share settled in renminbi rose from below 20% in 2021 to about 35%; per SAFE data the renminbi overtook the dollar in China's cross-border transactions in 2023 — [RBA Bulletin](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2026/aug/developments-in-rmb-internationalisation.html)
- 2024: renminbi used for 27% of China's goods trade and 32% of services trade — [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/econographics/the-imfs-boost-to-chinas-currency-is-costing-the-world-ten-years-later/). Goods trade settlement share about 12% to 13% in 2018 and close to 30% in 2026 — [IAI brief](https://www.iai.it/sites/default/files/iaibrief2644.pdf)
- CIPS: about CNY 175 trn processed in 2024, up over 40%; in 2025 more than 170 direct and over 1,500 indirect participants in about 120 countries and regions — [IAI brief](https://www.iai.it/sites/default/files/iaibrief2644.pdf). Daily average turnover above CNY 600 bn in 2024 and nearly CNY 800 bn in 2026; use up over 400% since 2020; CHIPS processed over 20 times CIPS's volume in 2025 — [RBA Bulletin](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2026/aug/developments-in-rmb-internationalisation.html)
- Shanghai's cross-border renminbi business topped CNY 20 trn in H1 2026 (headline only) — [Shanghai government](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-Latest-WhatsNew/20260804/5869bc396902418983cc2cfa233eff8d.html)

### Inferences
- The renminbi's reserve share is lower in 2025 to 2026 than at its 2022 peak even as its funding-currency role has grown. Borrowers are embracing it faster than reserve managers and private investors, which fits a low-yield currency: cheap to borrow, unrewarding to hold.
- The gap between the trade finance share (9.14%) and the payments share (2.38%) shows use is concentrated in China-related trade.

## What limits the renminbi's international role

### Takeaway
Official and academic sources agree on the same constraints: capital controls and incomplete convertibility, a managed exchange rate, shallow offshore markets and limited hedging access, a shortage of safe renminbi assets available to global investors, and low yields. Policy is widening access without touching convertibility.

### Cited Findings
- Constraints listed by the RBA: strong limits on capital outflows and a restrictive QDII quota; controls on converting offshore to onshore renminbi; priority on exchange-rate stability; restricted hedging access and underdeveloped markets; low bond yields and geopolitical risk reducing foreign demand since 2021; reserve holdings limited by capital controls, a shallower market than peers and low returns. Progress is likely to stay incremental without more capital account opening. The PBoC sold over USD 800 bn of reserves in 2014 to 2016, and offshore renminbi deposits fell over 25% from 2015 to 2017 after controls tightened — [RBA Bulletin, Aug 2026](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2026/aug/developments-in-rmb-internationalisation.html)
- IMF (2026), as quoted: "impediments remain to the widespread adoption" of the renminbi; offshore markets lack depth and liquidity; a shortage of globally accessible safe renminbi assets hinders its store-of-value role. A 2024 Federal Reserve note found exchange-rate management and capital controls keep it a niche currency. Beijing's control of the capital account is "little changed". The IMF estimated the renminbi undervalued by 17.3% to 25.3%. The next SDR basket review is due by July 2027 — [Atlantic Council, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/econographics/the-imfs-boost-to-chinas-currency-is-costing-the-world-ten-years-later/)
- "Its currency is not fully convertible"; June 2026 measures aim to bring in foreign investors "without touching convertibility"; a renminbi repo facility for foreign institutions is open to the official sector only; low onshore yields keep hedged returns unattractive — [IAI brief, 2026](https://www.iai.it/sites/default/files/iaibrief2644.pdf)
- The IMF Executive Board recommended "greater exchange rate flexibility"; the press release contains no statement on capital account opening — [IMF, 18 Feb 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/02/18/pr-26053-china-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation)
- Market view of the aim: "This policy's objective is not to topple the dollar but simply to ensure that the dollar-dominated system cannot be used against China" (Chi Lo, BNP Paribas Asset Management) — [Reuters reprint](https://gs.asiams.net/article_details/yuan-poised-to-overtake-dollar-for-chinese-overseas-bank-lending)
- The PBoC's 2026 to 2030 plan calls to "steadily advance high-level financial opening up" with no change to the monetary framework and a commitment to a "broadly stable" exchange rate — [BOFIT Weekly 36/2026](https://www.bofit.fi/en/monitoring/weekly/2026/vw202636_1/)
- Offshore renminbi liquidity pool about CNY 1 trn (about USD 150 bn, source conversion) — [China Daily HK, 14 Nov 2026](https://www.chinadailyhk.com/article/642680)

### Inferences
- The offshore liquidity pool of about CNY 1 trn is small beside a dim sum market of CNY 2.0 to 2.6 trn, which helps explain why issuance depends on southbound mainland money. Much "international" renminbi debt is therefore held by Chinese investors, which limits how far it represents foreign adoption.
- Foreigners hold 1.8% of the onshore market, so the renminbi lacks the large foreign-held safe-asset base that underpins the dollar's reserve role.
