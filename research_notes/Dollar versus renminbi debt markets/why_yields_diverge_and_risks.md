## 1. Why are Chinese yields low?

### Takeaway
China's 10-year yield is at or near a record low (about 1.6%, Nov 17, 2026) because of weak domestic demand, a continuing property slump, falling investment, PBoC rate cuts and a banking system and savings pool confined largely at home; the IMF, Koo, Pettis and Rhodium all read this as a symptom of a demand deficit, not as a cost advantage. One complication for the pure "deflation" story in late 2026: producer prices have swung sharply positive (energy and chip prices) while consumer inflation and yields stay very low.

### Cited Findings
- China's 10-year government bond yield was 1.62% on Nov 17, 2026, down 0.05 points over the month and 0.21 points over the year; the page's record low is 1.59% and a Nov 6, 2026 item described the yield as "holding at an all-time low." 12-month forecast on the page: 1.57%. — [Trading Economics, China 10Y](https://tradingeconomics.com/china/government-bond-yield)
- Drivers given on the same page (Nov 2026): the PBoC "accelerated interest rate reform in early November," setting an overnight policy rate at 1.25%, below the previous seven-day benchmark of 1.4%; a tech-sector equity selloff boosted bond demand; October 2026 retail sales grew 0.4% y/y, fixed-asset investment fell 8.1% year-to-date, new home prices fell 2.4%; CPI eased to 0.7% in October from 0.9%, while producer prices rose 5.7% (from 5%). — [Trading Economics, China 10Y](https://tradingeconomics.com/china/government-bond-yield) (aggregator; not checked against NBS/PBoC primary releases)
- Real GDP grew 4.5% y/y in Q3 2026 and 4.6% in the first three quarters; Q3 GDP at current prices was 375,367 (100 million yuan). The release does not state nominal growth or a deflator. — [National Bureau of Statistics, Oct 21, 2026](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202610/t20261021_1965671.html)
- September 2026 PPI was +5% y/y; goods retail sales rose 1.1% y/y in Q1–Q3 2026; exports in Q1–Q3 2026 were US$3.37 trillion, +23.8% y/y; September integrated-circuit exports rose 136.3% in value but 1.2% in volume, and the article warns trade values "may be distorted by rising chip prices." — [China Briefing, Q3 2026 GDP](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/china-q3-2026-gdp/)
- IMF (2025 Article IV, concluded Feb 18, 2026): deflationary pressures are "persistent"; private domestic demand is "lackluster"; GDP deflator −0.4% in 2024 and −0.7% in 2025; output gap −1.6% in 2025 and −1.0% in 2026; gross national saving 42.1% of GDP in 2025; current account surplus 3.3% of GDP in 2025. The IMF recommends "further monetary easing and greater exchange rate flexibility," shifting spending to consumption, and central-government financing to complete pre-sold housing. — [IMF press release 26/053](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/02/18/pr-26053-china-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation)
- Source conflict: the same IMF press release gives 2025 average headline CPI as 0 percent in the narrative but 1.8 in the table as extracted; the discrepancy is unexplained on the page (possible extraction error). — [IMF press release 26/053](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/02/18/pr-26053-china-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation)
- Richard Koo (Nomura Research Institute), interview published May 8, 2024: China's property bubble burst resembles Japan's about 34 years earlier; once asset prices stop rising people pay down debt "even at zero interest rates"; he read the then 2.4% government bond yield as a sign of excess savings relative to borrowing; monetary policy is ineffective without willing borrowers, so government should be borrower and spender of last resort. — [Yicai Global](https://www2.yicaiglobal.com/news/chinas-balance-sheet-recession-is-similar-and-different-to-that-of-japan-nris-richard-koo-says)
- State Street Global Advisors (undated; data to end-2024): 10-year CGB yield below 2.0% reflects weak demand, falling wages and investment, stalled consumption ("Japanification"), factory-gate deflation for more than two years, demographic decline, debt above 250% of GDP and potential growth slowing from about 5% to about 3%; the authors doubt supply-focused stimulus can fix a demand deficit. — [SSGA](https://ssga.com/us/en/individual/insights/why-chinese-government-bonds-warrant-attention-and-caution)
- Rhodium Group ("The Banks Behind the China Shock," Sept 10, 2026): a state-directed banking system misprices credit, keeps unproductive firms alive and contributes to deflation and trade surpluses. Evidence: 58% of new loans in December 2025 priced at or below the 3% Loan Prime Rate; in 2025 the median rate for central SOEs was 0.40 points below private firms (0.10 points in 2015); 18 of the 20 largest additions to corporate debt in 2025 went to state-owned borrowers; month-end bankers' acceptance discount rates fell as low as 0.01% in 2026, prompting regulators to tell some banks not to go below 0.5%. — [Rhodium Group](https://rhg.com/research/the-banks-behind-the-china-shock/)
- Capital controls / trapped savings: "Savings remain largely trapped behind capital controls"; regulators are cracking down on Hong Kong brokerages used to move money offshore (July 2026). Bruegel's Alicia García-Herrero (Mar 3, 2025) lists tighter capital controls than Japan had as a reason Chinese savings "cannot be deployed abroad." — [Semafor, Jul 21, 2026](https://semafor.com/article/07/21/2026/why-chinas-savers-want-out); [Bruegel](https://www.bruegel.org/newsletter/what-can-china-learn-japans-lost-decade)
- PBoC intervention to stop yields falling too far (2024 to 2025): the PBoC acquired about CNY1 trillion of CGBs between August and December 2024, concentrated in two- to seven-year maturities; one economist (Dong Ximiao) describes the 2024 approach as "buying short-term bonds and selling long-term paper." It then paused and resumed in October 2025 with a CNY20 billion operation, kept modest, per Dong, "to avoid a sharp drop in yields"; the 10-year was about 1.8% in mid-November 2025. PBoC CGB holdings were about 4.7% of its total assets. — [MNI, Nov 14, 2025](https://www.mnimarkets.com/mni-pboc-to-increase-cgb-purchases-add-longer-tenors-1763097613639)
- Search-result headlines (pages not successfully opened, so details unverified): SCMP "Record-low yields prompt suspension of government bond purchases by China's central bank" (Jan 2025) and "China's bull run in bonds triggers fresh warning from central bank amid record-low yields" (Dec 2024); Caixin "China central bank net bought 120 billion yuan of sovereign bonds in 2025" (Jan 15, 2026). — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3294282/record-low-yields-prompt-suspension-government-bond-purchases-chinas-central-bank); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3291391/chinas-bull-run-bonds-triggers-fresh-warning-central-bank-amid-record-low-yields); [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-01-15/china-central-bank-net-bought-120-billion-yuan-of-sovereign-bonds-in-2025-102404106.html)
- Offshore: a Feb 11, 2026 Ministry of Finance sale in Hong Kong (14 billion yuan) priced 2-year at 1.38%, 3-year at 1.4%, 5-year at 1.57%, the lowest coupons "since at least 2013," 3.94 times covered. — [Caixin, Feb 12, 2026](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-02-12/chinas-short-term-offshore-yuan-sovereign-yields-hit-decade-lows-102413954.html)

### Inferences
- The late-2026 data weaken the simplest "China is in deflation" framing: PPI is +5.7% (Oct 2026) and CPI positive, yet yields are at record lows. The pattern (weak retail sales, falling investment and home prices, cost-driven PPI, chip-price-inflated exports) fits imported cost inflation on top of weak domestic demand, which is still the Koo/IMF/Pettis diagnosis rather than a healthy low-rate environment.
- In 2024 to 2025 the PBoC acted to hold yields up (selling long bonds, pausing purchases, warnings); in November 2026 the reported policy-rate cut to 1.25% points the other way. I found no evidence of a 2026 PBoC effort to resist the latest record low.
- The "advantage" reading has some support in one respect: the Chinese state can direct a captive banking system to fund the government and favoured sectors at very low rates (Rhodium). The same source treats that as a distortion with costs, not a free lunch.

## 2. Why are US yields high in 2026?

### Takeaway
The US 10-year is about 5.1% (Nov 17, 2026), up about a percentage point in a year, chiefly because an oil shock from the Iran war pushed CPI to 3.7% and turned the Fed under Kevin Warsh from expected cuts to hikes; structural deficits near 6% of GDP, a positive term premium, rating-agency warnings and softer foreign official demand sit underneath.

### Cited Findings
- US 10-year Treasury yield 5.10% on Nov 17, 2026, up 0.99 points from a year earlier; near 5.15% on Nov 16. Markets price nearly 95% odds of a 25bp hike in December, with more expected in January and April; St. Louis Fed President Musalem said as many as four hikes may be needed. Fed funds rate 4.00% in October 2026; CPI inflation 3.70% in September 2026 (from 3.40%); crude near $86.5 and Brent near $93.4; unemployment 4.10% in October 2026. Treasuries "did not attract safe-haven demand" during an AI-infrastructure stock selloff. — [Trading Economics, US 10Y](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield) (aggregator)
- The Fed raised rates on Sept 16, 2026 by unanimous vote under Chair Kevin Warsh (who succeeded Jerome Powell in 2026); its statement dropped the earlier reference to supply shocks; nearly all policymakers signalled a second hike this year. The 10-year topped 5% that week for the first time since 2023; 30-year mortgage rate just below 7%; gasoline $4.44 a gallon, diesel a record $6.40; the Iran war was in its seventh month; federal debt "recently topped $40 trillion"; AI data-center bond issuance by large tech firms "has also pushed rates up." Size of hike and new rate level not stated. — [AP via Food Manufacturing, Sept 18, 2026](https://www.foodmanufacturing.com/supply-chain/news/22974742/why-the-federal-reserve-is-lifting-rates-now-and-what-it-means)
- As of May 18, 2026, CME FedWatch showed about 49% probability of a hike in December; cause cited was continued disruption in the Strait of Hormuz. Warsh "has signaled support for lower rates" but has one of 12 votes. — [Yahoo Finance, May 18, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/fed-rate-hike-odds-rise-200002875.html)
- Term premium: NY Fed ACM 10-year term premium was +0.83% on May 15, 2026 (+0.73% for April 2026), versus −0.10% on Sept 30, 2024 and a series low of −1.34% in July 2020; the full-series median is 1.41%, so April 2026 was about the 35th percentile. Expected-rate component 3.85% on May 15, 2026. — [eco3min, citing NY Fed ACM data](https://eco3min.fr/en/acmtp10-term-premium-10-year-treasury-yield-decomposition/) (third-party republication)
- Deficits and supply (CBO Feb 2026 baseline, via CRFB): deficit $1,775 billion (5.8% of GDP) in FY2025, $1,853 billion (5.8%) in FY2026, $3,115 billion (6.7%) in FY2036; $24.4 trillion cumulative over the decade. CBO assumed the 10-year yield would average 4.1% in 2026 and 4.4% by 2031. — [CRFB summary of CBO Feb 2026 outlook](https://www.crfb.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/CRFB_CBO_February_2026_Budget_And_Economic_Outlook_4.pdf)
- Tariffs: CRFB estimates the Supreme Court's 2026 ruling against most of the Trump tariffs could cost $1.7 trillion in revenue through 2036 (a fiscal-supply channel rather than an inflation channel). — [Fortune, May 3, 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/05/03/the-us-is-in-a-league-of-its-own-when-it-comes-to-its-debt-burden-as-rating-agencies-bemoan-long-running-deterioration-in-fiscal-governance/)
- Ratings: Fitch AA+/Stable (downgraded from AAA in 2023); on April 30, 2026 Fitch warned the rating could slip further and projected a general government deficit of 7.9% of GDP in 2026 and 2027: "Structurally large fiscal deficits will keep the U.S.'s debt burden far above that of other 'AA' category sovereigns." Moody's cut from Aaa to Aa1 in 2025. No new 2026 downgrade is reported in that article. — [Fortune, May 3, 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/05/03/the-us-is-in-a-league-of-its-own-when-it-comes-to-its-debt-burden-as-rating-agencies-bemoan-long-running-deterioration-in-fiscal-governance/)
- Foreign demand: total foreign holdings of Treasuries were US$9.248 trillion in July 2026, a second monthly fall but up 1.5% from a year earlier. China held US$618 billion, the lowest since September 2008 and down more than 11% y/y; Japan US$1.104 trillion (peak US$1.325 trillion, Nov 2021); UK US$998.3 billion. — [The Standard (HK), TIC data for July 2026](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/343058/Foreign-holdings-of-US-Treasuries-in-July-down-for-2nd-straight-month-data-shows)
- IMF Fiscal Monitor, April 2026 (executive summary): "supply-driven increases in US yields spill over almost one for one to foreign bond markets" and the US Treasury safety premium is eroding. IMF GFSR, October 2026 (executive summary): resurgent inflation or fiscal concerns could push sovereign yields sharply higher in a "disorderly manner." — [IMF Fiscal Monitor Apr 2026](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/fiscal-monitor/2026/april/english/execsum.pdf.md); [IMF GFSR Oct 2026](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/gfsr/2026/october/english/execsum.pdf.md)

### Inferences
- The 2026 rise in US yields looks mostly like an expected-policy-rate and inflation story (oil shock, Fed hiking) layered on a term premium that had already turned positive in 2025. The May 2026 ACM reading (+0.83%) is positive but below its long-run median, which argues against attributing the bulk of a 5%+ yield to a fiscal risk premium; the yield was 4.45% in April and is 5.10% now, and I found no ACM value after May 15 to split that later move.
- Actual 2026 yields (5.1%) are about a point above CBO's February 2026 assumption for the year (4.1%), so CBO's interest-cost path is likely understated if yields stay here.
- Foreign demand: aggregate foreign holdings are still up year on year; it is Chinese official holdings that are falling. "Reduced foreign demand" is true for China's reserve manager, not clearly true in aggregate.

## 3. Debt sustainability: which country's debt is growing faster, and is r below g in China?

### Takeaway
On broad measures China's debt is larger and rising faster relative to GDP than America's (IMF augmented debt up almost 9 points of GDP in one year versus about 2 points for US debt held by the public), but China funds it at about 1.6% in its own closed system while the US pays about 5% and CBO already expects its average interest rate to exceed growth later this decade.

### Cited Findings
- US: debt held by the public 99% of GDP in FY2025, 101% in FY2026, 120% by FY2036 (CBO Feb 2026). Net interest $970 billion in FY2025, "a record 3.2% of GDP," rising to $2.1 trillion (4.6% of GDP) in FY2036. CRFB: "the average interest rate on all federal debt will exceed nominal economic growth (R>G)" later in the decade, which "could represent the start of a debt spiral." — [CRFB summary of CBO Feb 2026 outlook](https://www.crfb.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/CRFB_CBO_February_2026_Budget_And_Economic_Outlook_4.pdf)
- US debt held by the public (about $31.27 trillion) surpassed annual GDP ($31.22 trillion) in March 2026, the first time since World War II (CRFB, announced April 30, 2026). — [Fortune, May 3, 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/05/03/the-us-is-in-a-league-of-its-own-when-it-comes-to-its-debt-burden-as-rating-agencies-bemoan-long-running-deterioration-in-fiscal-governance/)
- China (IMF Article IV, Feb 2026): general government debt 60.9% of GDP in 2024, 68.4% in 2025, 75.1% in 2026, 86.9% in 2030. Augmented debt (includes LGFVs and other off-budget borrowing) 126.6% in 2025, 135.3% in 2026, 153.7% in 2030. Augmented deficit 14.3% of GDP in 2025 and 14.2% in 2026. Household debt 59.4% of GDP (2025); non-financial corporate domestic debt 116% (2025) and 122% (2026). — [IMF press release 26/053](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/02/18/pr-26053-china-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation)
- IMF WEO April 2026 gross general government debt (as reported by Mappr): US 125.8% of GDP in 2026 and 142.1% in 2031 (+16.3 points); China 106.9% in 2026 and 126.8% in 2031 (+19.9 points); Japan 204.4% falling to 192.8%. Mappr says much of China's increase reflects reclassification of LGFV liabilities. The IMF Fiscal Monitor April 2026 summary confirms 142% (US) and 127% (China) by 2031, with a US deficit of 7% to 8% of GDP and China's "nearly 8%". — [Mappr](https://www.mappr.co/public-debt-by-country/); [IMF Fiscal Monitor Apr 2026](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/fiscal-monitor/2026/april/english/execsum.pdf.md)
- Definition conflict to flag: China's 2026 "general government debt" is 75.1% in the Article IV press-release table but 106.9% in the WEO as reported by Mappr; the two use different perimeters and neither equals augmented debt (135.3%).
- BIS total credit to the non-financial sector, 2026-Q1: China 305.2% of GDP, United States 250.5%, Japan 349.4%. — [statisticsoftheworld.com, BIS series](https://statisticsoftheworld.com/series/BIS.CREDIT.TOTAL.GDP.Q) (third-party republication; not checked on bis.org)
- Pettis (Carnegie, Aug 25, 2026): China's debt has grown at perhaps the fastest pace in history over 10 to 15 years and is "second only to Japan among the major economies"; "There is no painless solution." — [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/china-financial-markets/2026/08/who-paid-for-chinas-last-debt-cleanup-and-who-will-pay-for-the-next)
- Global context: gross government debt nearly 94% of GDP in 2025, projected 100% by 2029; global interest payments rose from 2% to nearly 3% of GDP over four years. — [IMF Fiscal Monitor Apr 2026](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/fiscal-monitor/2026/april/english/execsum.pdf.md)

### Inferences
- Pace (my calculations from the cited tables): China's augmented debt rises 8.7 points of GDP from 2025 to 2026 and 27.1 points from 2025 to 2030; US debt held by the public rises 2 points from FY2025 to FY2026 and 9 points to FY2030. China's debt ratio is growing several times faster on the broad measure.
- US interest as a share of revenue: not stated in the sources read. A derived estimate (FY2025 net interest $970 billion over revenue of about $5,235 billion taken from the CRFB chart) gives about 18.5%, and about 25% for FY2036; treat as approximate and unverified against CBO tables.
- Is China's r below g? For 2025, IMF real growth of 5.0% and a deflator of −0.7% imply nominal growth of roughly 4.3% (my calculation), against a 10-year yield of about 1.8% in November 2025. So even with deflation, the sovereign rate was well below nominal growth. The debt ratio still rises fast because the augmented primary deficit is enormous (augmented deficit about 14% of GDP). A favourable r minus g does not stabilise debt when the deficit is that large. For LGFVs and firms borrowing at 3% or more, the margin over roughly 4% nominal growth is thin.
- For the US the position is the mirror image: smaller deficit (about 6% of GDP) but a marginal borrowing rate near 5% that is at or above plausible nominal growth, which is the condition CBO/CRFB warn about.

## 4. Historical parallel: Japan after 1990

### Takeaway
Analysts who use the Japan parallel argue that very low yields there accompanied two decades of private deleveraging and stagnation rather than an investment boom; they also list real differences (China is poorer, still urbanising, has tighter capital controls, a weaker safety net, a bigger construction overhang and faces US technology containment). I did not find a source with Japan's yield series, so no JGB yield numbers are given.

### Cited Findings
- Koo (May 2024): Japan's recovery took about 20 years; borrowers pay down debt "even at zero interest rates." Differences: "Everyone in China knows about the balance sheet recession," so policy can respond sooner; Japan's was a purely financial bubble whereas construction is "now 26 percent of China's GDP," so the output hit is direct; China's balance-sheet problem is probably smaller but combined with the construction slump could be "quite challenging." — [Yicai Global](https://www2.yicaiglobal.com/news/chinas-balance-sheet-recession-is-similar-and-different-to-that-of-japan-nris-richard-koo-says)
- García-Herrero (Bruegel, Mar 3, 2025): similarities are high savings, very low consumption, over-investment, persistent current account surpluses, and a post-bubble doubling down on industrial policy that in Japan "did not stop deceleration" while overcapacity deepened deflation. Differences: China's consumption share is lower than Japan's was; no developed welfare state; tighter capital controls; Japan had large foreign creditor income; stronger US containment; in China's favour it is poorer (more convergence room) and still urbanising, with ageing effects stronger after 2035. — [Bruegel](https://www.bruegel.org/newsletter/what-can-china-learn-japans-lost-decade)
- SSGA: Japan's "lost decades" followed 1990 tightening and "large fiscal stimulus did not prevent this period"; Japan's debt-to-GDP is about 250%, similar to China's total debt; a chart shows China's long-term borrowing costs falling below Japan's. — [SSGA](https://ssga.com/us/en/individual/insights/why-chinese-government-bonds-warrant-attention-and-caution)
- IMF WEO April 2026 (via Mappr): Japan's gross debt is projected to fall from 204.4% of GDP (2026) to 192.8% (2031), attributed to inflation and nominal GDP growth eroding the ratio. — [Mappr](https://www.mappr.co/public-debt-by-country/)
- Counter-view exists: a 2023 Bloomberg headline reads "Chinese Economists Push Back On 'Balance Sheet Recession' Risk" (article not opened). — [Bloomberg, Jul 19, 2023](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-07-19/chinese-economists-push-back-on-balance-sheet-recession-risk)

### Inferences
- The Japan case cuts both ways for the "cheap borrowing is an advantage" reading: low yields did let Japan carry very high public debt for decades without a funding crisis, but they were a sign that private borrowers would not borrow. Japan's debt ratio is now falling only because inflation and nominal growth returned.
- China today differs from early-1990s Japan in exporting its way through: record trade surpluses (see section 6) are doing work that domestic demand is not, which Japan could do less of at a comparable stage.

### Gaps
- JGB 10-year yield path after 1990 and Japanese investment/GDP data: not found in a source read this session.
- A detailed statement of the Chinese economists' rebuttal to Koo: not found (article not opened).

## 5. Who bears the cost of low Chinese yields? Is capital trying to leave?

### Takeaway
The costs fall on bank margins (record-low 1.4%), insurers (duration gap over 10 years, regulatory forbearance and state capital), and household savers (Pettis: the historic recapitaliser of the banks through repressed deposit rates). There is clear evidence of money trying to leave and of tightened controls, though Setser argues the net pressure on the renminbi is to appreciate.

### Cited Findings
- Banks: commercial banks' net interest margin was 1.4% at end-March 2026, a record low, down 2bp from end-2025; Q1 2026 profit fell 3.7% y/y (NFRA data). — [Caixin, May 18, 2026](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-05-18/chinese-banks-net-interest-margin-hits-record-low-102444983.html)
- In 2026 the government injected about RMB 290 billion into its banks. — [Rhodium Group, Sept 10, 2026](https://rhg.com/research/the-banks-behind-the-china-shock/)
- Insurers: the regulator dropped a draft rule requiring close asset-liability duration matching, replacing it with a "hedging ratio" and a three-year transition (rules issued mid-August 2026); the industry duration gap "topped 10 years in 2024." — [Caixin, Aug 24, 2026](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-08-24/china-throws-insurers-lifeline-as-falling-rates-squeeze-returns-102477460.html)
- Search-result headlines only (not opened): "China Injects 360 Billion Yuan Into Financial Sector as Low Bond Yields Squeeze Major Insurers"; Caixin "China weighs lower cap on life insurance return projections" (Mar 27, 2026). — [edgeX news](https://pro.edgex.exchange/en-US/news/article/china-360-billion-yuan-state-insurers-special-bonds); [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-03-27/exclusive-china-weighs-lower-cap-on-life-insurance-return-projections-102428353.html)
- Households: Pettis says that in 2001 to 2010 deposit rates far below nominal GDP growth transferred as much as 5 percentage points of GDP per year from savers; "households, largely without realizing it, recapitalized the financial system"; household consumption fell from 63.9% of GDP in 2000 to 49.4% in 2010. He argues passing losses to households "is now much less available"; the alternatives are businesses or government absorbing them. — [Carnegie, Aug 25, 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/china-financial-markets/2026/08/who-paid-for-chinas-last-debt-cleanup-and-who-will-pay-for-the-next)
- Savers today: "The government keeps bank interest rates low to support corporate borrowers, which penalizes depositors"; property prices are down about 30% from peak. Bank of China pays 0.8% on a one-year US dollar time deposit "and still attracts funds." — [Semafor, Jul 21, 2026](https://semafor.com/article/07/21/2026/why-chinas-savers-want-out); [Desjardins, Sept 24, 2026](https://www.desjardins.com/content/dam/pdf/en/personal/savings-investment/economic-studies/china-bonds-24-september-2026.pdf)
- Capital leaving: "Up to $1 trillion is thought to have left China last year" (2025); regulators are cracking down on Hong Kong brokerages; HSBC limited mainland travel for Hong Kong private bankers (per Bloomberg, cited by Semafor). Balance-of-payments outward portfolio investment totalled US$1.3 trillion over the last 12 months (Desjardins, which doubts it is all private given a QDII quota of about US$183 billion). J.P. Morgan's Michael Cembalest: large outflows could "crush the RMB and trigger a collapse in Chinese equity/real estate markets." — [Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/07/21/2026/why-chinas-savers-want-out); [Desjardins](https://www.desjardins.com/content/dam/pdf/en/personal/savings-investment/economic-studies/china-bonds-24-september-2026.pdf)
- Opposing view: Brad Setser (CFR): "The preponderance of evidence suggests that China has been resisting pressure on its currency to appreciate"; trade-surplus inflows exceed the desire to move money out. Desjardins agrees the "pain trade" is for the renminbi to rise. Adam Tooze: "more financial repression is the unmistakable implication of calling for revaluation." — [Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/07/21/2026/why-chinas-savers-want-out); [Desjardins](https://www.desjardins.com/content/dam/pdf/en/personal/savings-investment/economic-studies/china-bonds-24-september-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- Low yields are in part a transfer: borrowers (government, SOEs, LGFVs) gain what depositors, bank shareholders and policyholders lose. Because the state owns most banks and large insurers, part of the cost returns to the public balance sheet as recapitalisations (RMB 290 billion for banks in 2026).
- Gross outflow pressure (households, state banks buying foreign bonds) and net appreciation pressure (record surplus) can both be true; the two camps are describing gross versus net flows.

## 6. Implications for global investors

### Takeaway
Foreign investors have not rotated from Treasuries into onshore Chinese bonds; they sold Chinese bonds for 11 straight months to March 2026. The observable diversification is into gold (now a larger share of official reserves than Treasuries) and euro assets, while the renminbi's growing role is as a cheap borrowing currency.

### Cited Findings
- Foreign investors were net sellers of yuan bonds for 11 straight months through March 2026, the longest run since the data began; holdings in the interbank market fell by 1.25 trillion yuan (about $183 billion, −28%) to 3.19 trillion yuan. — [Bloomberg, Apr 16, 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-16/foreign-investors-keep-selling-chinese-bonds-despite-resilience) (paywalled; summary only)
- Gold: at end-2025 gold was 27% of global official reserves (20% a year earlier) versus 22% for US Treasuries (25% a year earlier); euro 15%; all dollar assets 42% (ECB report, June 2, 2026). Central banks bought 850 tonnes in 2025, down from over 1,000 tonnes in each of the three prior years; total holdings over 36,000 tonnes. World Gold Council (January 2026): foreign central bank gold approaching $4 trillion versus about $3.9 trillion in Treasuries, the first such crossover since 1996. Turkey sold or loaned 130 tonnes in early 2026. — [Northern Miner, citing ECB](https://www.northernminer.com/news/gold-overtakes-us-treasuries-in-global-reserve-shift-ecb/1003891748/)
- Search-result headline only: "Record 45% of central banks plan to increase gold holdings, WGC survey finds" (Kitco, Jun 16, 2026; not opened). — [Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-06-16/record-45-central-banks-plan-increase-gold-holdings-wgc-survey-finds)
- Euro: euro-denominated international debt issuance rose 30% in 2025 to nearly €1 trillion; net foreign investment in euro-area assets was €850 billion. — [Northern Miner, citing ECB](https://www.northernminer.com/news/gold-overtakes-us-treasuries-in-global-reserve-shift-ecb/1003891748/)
- China's own diversification: official reserves rose only US$115 billion over the last four quarters against a current account surplus of US$826 billion; state banks absorbed the rest ("shadow reserves" of about US$710 billion a year, midpoint). Bank of China's foreign bond portfolio grew from US$140 billion (2019) to US$340 billion (2025). The dollar share of Chinese banks' foreign assets fell from about 80% (2022) to 68% (2026). The author's conclusion: "not a wholesale liquidation of Treasuries" but a shift to a more price-sensitive, duration-hedging buyer. IMF COFER: dollar share of global reserves fell from 62% to 57% since COVID-19. — [Desjardins, Sept 24, 2026](https://www.desjardins.com/content/dam/pdf/en/personal/savings-investment/economic-studies/china-bonds-24-september-2026.pdf)
- Renminbi as a funding currency: yuan notes and loans totalled about US$167 billion in 2025, roughly triple five years earlier; record 218 billion yuan of yuan bonds in 2026 to March 6; dim sum issuance 103 billion yuan (nearly double the 2025 period); panda issuance 51.4 billion yuan; offshore yuan loans up more than 4.5 times to 42.5 billion yuan in 2025. Indonesia's 10-year offshore yuan note (Feb 2026) priced about one point below its eight-year euro tranche. Lower Chinese rates "amid persistent deflation" are cited as the driver. Yuan share of global reserves below 2% (Q3 2025). — [The Edge Singapore (Bloomberg)](https://alfi.dev.theedgesingapore.com/news/china/yuan-funding-hits-record-us200-billion-chinas-ambitions-grow)
- Dim sum bonds outstanding were about RMB1.6 trillion (US$230 billion) at end-2025, up about 60% in three years. — [Desjardins](https://www.desjardins.com/content/dam/pdf/en/personal/savings-investment/economic-studies/china-bonds-24-september-2026.pdf)
- Carry trade history: from Q4 2023 foreigners bought short-dated onshore CGBs and swapped to dollars for a pickup of 100bp or more over SOFR swaps; by October 2024 that trade was being unwound. — [FX Markets, Oct 21, 2024](https://www.fx-markets.com/node/7949417)
- Investor case for CGBs: USD-hedged CGBs outperformed major peers over the 20 years to end-2024 with low volatility; CGBs were over 10% of the Bloomberg Global Aggregate at end-2024; the caution is geopolitical (Taiwan "Grey Swan", Russia-style sanctions), which "may be too large for a yield under 2.0%." — [SSGA](https://ssga.com/us/en/individual/insights/why-chinese-government-bonds-warrant-attention-and-caution)
- Renminbi limits: capital controls keep it from challenging the dollar's reserve status since "foreign holders need free access to their funds" (Andy Browne). Iran has reportedly let some tankers through Hormuz on condition of renminbi settlement (Zakaria, reported by Semafor; single-source and second-hand). — [Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/07/21/2026/why-chinas-savers-want-out)

### Inferences
- Low renminbi yields make the currency attractive to borrow, not to hold: issuance is booming while foreign holdings of onshore bonds shrink. That is the funding-currency pattern, though I found no source in 2026 explicitly documenting a large renminbi-funded carry trade.
- With a spread of about 3.5 points between US and Chinese 10-year yields (5.10% less 1.62%, my calculation), an unhedged foreign holder of CGBs gives up substantial carry and needs renminbi appreciation to compensate; Setser and Desjardins think that appreciation pressure is real.
- The beneficiaries of diversification away from Treasuries so far are gold and the euro, not renminbi bonds.

## 7. What would have to change for the yield relationship to reverse?

### Takeaway
No source read gives an explicit reversal scenario; from the evidence, narrowing would require either a durable revival of Chinese domestic demand and credit appetite (or a forced opening of the capital account), or a US disinflation and Fed easing cycle with fiscal consolidation. Forecasts on hand imply only marginal narrowing over 12 months.

### Cited Findings
- Model forecasts: China 10-year 1.57% in 12 months; US 10-year 4.88% in 12 months. — [Trading Economics China](https://tradingeconomics.com/china/government-bond-yield); [Trading Economics US](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield)
- IMF prescription for China: expansionary policy until deflationary pressure subsides durably, a shift to consumption and stronger social protection to cut precautionary saving, and greater exchange-rate flexibility; IMF projects the output gap closing by 2029 and real growth slowing to 3.4% by 2030. — [IMF press release 26/053](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/02/18/pr-26053-china-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation)
- Koo: fiscal policy is the key; the government must borrow and spend the private sector's excess savings; the process could last "five or seven" years. — [Yicai Global](https://www2.yicaiglobal.com/news/chinas-balance-sheet-recession-is-similar-and-different-to-that-of-japan-nris-richard-koo-says)
- Pettis: resolving bad debt means businesses absorb losses "through higher wages, higher interest rates, currency appreciation," or government does through transfers and asset sales. — [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/china-financial-markets/2026/08/who-paid-for-chinas-last-debt-cleanup-and-who-will-pay-for-the-next)
- US side: the September 2026 hike reflected the Fed no longer treating the energy shock as temporary; a Truist analyst in May 2026 said hikes would need oil sustained near 2022 post-invasion levels. IMF GFSR (Oct 2026) recommends putting public debt on a sustainable path and preserving central bank independence to reduce the risk of a sharp rise in yields. — [AP via Food Manufacturing](https://www.foodmanufacturing.com/supply-chain/news/22974742/why-the-federal-reserve-is-lifting-rates-now-and-what-it-means); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/fed-rate-hike-odds-rise-200002875.html); [IMF GFSR Oct 2026](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/gfsr/2026/october/english/execsum.pdf.md)
- Japan precedent for the low-yield side changing: Japan's debt ratio is projected to fall as inflation and nominal growth return. — [Mappr](https://www.mappr.co/public-debt-by-country/)

### Inferences
- Chinese yields would rise if (a) household income and consumption are lifted enough to restore private credit demand (IMF, Pettis), (b) fiscal issuance outruns the banking system's captive demand, (c) capital controls loosen so that savings can chase 5% dollar yields, or (d) cost-push PPI inflation feeds into CPI and forces the PBoC to tighten. The first three are policy choices Beijing has so far avoided; the fourth is now a live data question given PPI at +5.7%.
- US yields would fall if the Middle East energy shock ends and the Fed reverses, or in a growth/AI-equity bust that restores safe-haven demand; though the November 2026 episode in which Treasuries did not rally during an equity selloff is a warning that the safe-haven reflex is weaker.
- A full reversal (Chinese yields above US yields, as was normal before 2022) would need both sides to move; nothing read this session forecasts that.
