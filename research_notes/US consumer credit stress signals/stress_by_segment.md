## 1. Auto loans: subprime delinquency, repossessions, negative equity, payments. At or beyond record levels in 2026?

### Takeaway
Auto is the clearest "beyond 2008 to 2010" segment on several measures: Fitch's subprime 60+ day index set an all-time high of 6.90% in January 2026, the New York Fed's 90+ day share of auto balances (5.66%, Q3 2026) is above its post-2008 peak of about 5%, and payment, term and negative-equity measures are at or near records. But the 2026 direction is mixed rather than uniformly worsening: the Fitch subprime index fell to 6.13% by July 2026, Cox says subprime 60+ was a full point better year on year in August 2026, and flow into serious delinquency is flat (3.02% vs 2.99%); the worsening is in the stock of seriously delinquent debt, in early-stage delinquency, and in prime.

### Cited Findings
Subprime delinquency
- Fitch subprime auto ABS 60+ day delinquency was 6.90% in January 2026 (December 2025 collection period), the highest in the index's roughly 32-year history under the methodology then in effect; pandemic low was 2.58% (May 2021); June 2026 5.80%; July 2026 6.13%. Fitch revised its index inclusion methodology effective with the July 2026 release and restated history, so the originally published January figure and later figures are not strictly comparable (secondary source citing Auto Remarketing/Fitch) — [ESP Data, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.espdata.com/subprime-auto-loan-delinquencies-2026/)
- Earlier record points: October 2025 6.65% ("highest on record" at the time), September 2025 6.50%, a year before that 6.23%; prime 0.37% (page undated; the figures match October 2025) — [BadCredit.org](https://www.badcredit.org/news/subprime-auto-delinquencies-hit-6-65-percent-raising-loss-risks/)
- Fitch prime 60-day index 0.49% in July 2026 vs 0.42% a year earlier; it reached only about 0.9% at the bottom of the Great Recession (article cites Wolf Street) — [Yahoo Finance / The Auto Wire, 21 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/repo-watch-good-credit-got-220000409.html)
- Cox Automotive (September 2026 forecast call, August 2026 data): "Delinquencies fell across nearly every tier in August. Subprime 60-plus delinquencies were a full percentage point better year over year"; credit performance is "the offset to watch" and "for now it is behaving" — [Cox Automotive Q3 2026 Sales Forecast Update transcript](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Q3-2026-Cox-Automotive-Sales-Forecast-Update-Transcript.pdf)
- Conflicting, different dataset: LoanTape (securitized subprime pools, September 2026) shows 30+ days late at 17.68%, the highest September reading since its data began in 2017, and 60+ at 7%, versus 11.02% and 3.43% in September 2019; the 30-day rate rose 153 bp year on year, of which 101 bp came from pool composition and 52 bp remained after controlling for issuer and pool age; delinquencies rose at 10 of the 12 major issuers with at least $500M of subprime balances — [24/7 Wall St., 2 Nov 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/02/auto-delinquencies-are-near-a-10-year-high-and-it-could-get-a-lot-worse/)

- Flow into serious (90+ day) delinquency, auto: 3.02% annualized in Q3 2026 vs 2.99% in Q3 2025 — [NY Fed press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110)
- Share of auto balances 90+ days past due: 5.66% in Q3 2026, up from 5.49% in Q2 2026 and 5.02% in Q3 2025; seriously delinquent auto balances about $98.1bn vs $83.1bn a year earlier (+$15bn, about 18%); transition into 30+ day delinquency 7.96% in Q3 2026 vs 7.79% in Q3 2025; the article says 5.66% is above "the approximately 5% level reached during the aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis" (secondary source relaying NY Fed data) — [CU Collector, 15 Nov 2026](https://blog.cucollector.com/seriously-delinquent-auto-loan-debt-jumps-15-billion-as-borrower-distress-persists/)

- NY Fed text: early delinquency transitions for auto "ticked up" in Q3 2026, serious transitions "remained flat"; $208bn of new auto loans; origination credit quality "largely steady" — [NY Fed HHDC 2026Q3](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/pdf/hhdc_2026q3.pdf)

Repossessions

- Edmunds Q3 2026 (via secondary): 21.2% of new-vehicle loans carry a payment of $1,000 or more (19.1% a year earlier); 25.5% of new loans run 84+ months (21.8% a year earlier); average new-car loan $44,664 at 7.0% for 70.5 months, average payment $787 — [Yahoo Finance / The Auto Wire, 21 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/repo-watch-good-credit-got-220000409.html)
- Cox/Dealertrack August 2026: 57.4% of loans written with negative equity, higher than any monthly reading from 2015 to 2019; average new-loan payment $802 year to date, the first time above $800 ($782 last year); $1,000-plus payments 17.8% of loans vs 13.3% in 2022; under-$500 payments under 21% vs nearly 30% in 2022; loans longer than 72 months a record 31.3%, up 580 bp year over year; average used-loan payment near $540; average new contract rate 10.99% — [Cox Automotive transcript](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Q3-2026-Cox-Automotive-Sales-Forecast-Update-Transcript.pdf)
- Cox macro context (2026): gas prices up 41% year over year, national average near $4.50; expenditure growth nearly 6% year over year through July vs 3.7% income growth, the widest gap since 2021; real disposable income +0.5% year over year as of July — [Cox Automotive transcript](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Q3-2026-Cox-Automotive-Sales-Forecast-Update-Transcript.pdf)
- Credit is loosening, not tightening: Dealertrack Credit Availability Index 105.3 in August 2026, the highest since November 2015; subprime share of approvals 16.6% (Cox calls it a mix effect) — [Cox Automotive transcript](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Q3-2026-Cox-Automotive-Sales-Forecast-Update-Transcript.pdf). NY Fed originations to the 620 to 659 score band rose 55% in a year and the median score on new auto loans fell to 716 from 724 (period not stated; via Wards Auto) — [Yahoo Finance / The Auto Wire, 21 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/repo-watch-good-credit-got-220000409.html)

### Inferences
- "Record" is accurate for: Fitch subprime 60+ (January 2026 peak), NY Fed 90+ share of auto balances (5.66% Q3 2026 exceeds the ~5% post-2008 peak per the secondary source), share of loans over 72 months, average payment, and Manheim repo volume at auction. It is not accurate for prime delinquency (0.49% vs ~0.9% in the Great Recession) or for confirmed repossession counts (latest Cox estimate still below 2009).
- The stock of seriously delinquent auto debt is rising (+18% year on year) while the flow is flat, which points to loans staying delinquent longer or slower charge-off/repossession resolution rather than an accelerating inflow.
- Why it is bad: high vehicle prices and rates (payments +47% vs 2019 for rollover buyers), 2022 to 2023 vintages bought at peak prices now underwater, plus looser underwriting and new subprime issuers shifting pool mix (LoanTape attributes two-thirds of the year-on-year rise to composition).
- Negative equity on 57% of new loans with record terms is a forward-looking loss-severity risk if unemployment rises.

## 2. Student loans: delinquency, default, credit scores, share behind

### Takeaway
Student debt is the segment with the largest and fastest deterioration since 2024, but the inflow has peaked: flow into serious delinquency halved to 7.72% in Q3 2026 from 14.26% a year earlier, while the stock keeps building (10.9% of balances 90+ days past due, 9.3 million borrowers and $234bn in default at June 2026). The stock is roughly back at pre-pandemic levels rather than above them; the damage is concentrated in credit scores (average fall of 91 points for defaulters) and in spillover to those borrowers' other debts.

### Cited Findings
- Student loan balances 90+ days past due: 10.9% in Q3 2026, up from 10.6% in Q2 2026; balances $1.656tn — [NY Fed HHDC 2026Q3](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/pdf/HHDC_2026Q3)
- Flow into serious delinquency, student loans: 7.72% annualized in Q3 2026 vs 14.26% in Q3 2025 — [NY Fed press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110); quarter on quarter it eased to 7.7% from 7.8% — [NAHB Eye on Housing](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/11/household-debt-delinquencies-rise-as-mortgage-performance-weakens/)
- Serious delinquency is "back above 10%," where it was before the pandemic — [Wolf Street, 11 Nov 2026](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/11/11/household-debts-debt-to-income-ratios-delinquencies-foreclosures-collections-bankruptcies-in-q3-2026/)
- New York Fed, data through Q1 2026: roughly 1 million federal borrowers defaulted in Q4 2025 and another 2.6 million in Q1 2026 (about 3.6 million); over 17% of student loan borrowers have been 90+ days past due at least once since Q1 2025; SAVE plan borrowers (about 7 million) could produce a second default wave; delinquent and newly defaulted borrowers are about 2% of the credit population — [Liberty Street Economics, 12 May 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/federal-student-loan-defaults-return-after-pa)
- Credit scores: average score of defaulted borrowers fell 91 points, from 567 (Q3 2024) to 476 (Q4 2025), Equifax Risk Score 3.0 — [Liberty Street Economics, 12 May 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/federal-student-loan-defaults-return-after-pa)
- Spillover (Q1 2026): among defaulted borrowers, nearly 40% were past due on auto loans, 56% on credit cards, 20% on mortgages; defaulted or delinquent student borrowers hold 2.7% of auto balances, 2% of card balances and 1% of mortgage balances — [Liberty Street Economics, 12 May 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/federal-student-loan-defaults-return-after-pa)
- Who: recent defaulters average 38.9 years old vs 36.4 for pre-pause defaulters (Q2 2018 to Q4 2019), with a shift toward borrowers 50 and older; at least 10% of borrowers defaulted in Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia and South Carolina; every state had at least 4% — [Liberty Street Economics, 12 May 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/federal-student-loan-defaults-return-after-pa)
- Department of Education (Federal Student Aid data released September 2026, as of June 2026): over 9.3 million borrowers in default, up about 400,000 in the quarter; $234bn in default, about 14% of the $1.64tn federal portfolio; of 17.4 million borrowers in repayment or delinquency, nearly 20% (about 3.5 million) are more than 30 days delinquent and about 1.5 million are in late-stage delinquency at risk of default within six months. Advocates' letter: 25% of borrowers behind, 1 in 5 in default — [Forbes, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/adamminsky/2026/10/05/234-billion-in-student-loans-are-now-in-default-and-things-are-about-to-get-worse/)
- SAVE wind-down: servicers began sending notices in July 2026 giving 90 days to pick another plan; first borrowers hit the deadline in late September 2026 — [Forbes, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/adamminsky/2026/10/05/234-billion-in-student-loans-are-now-in-default-and-things-are-about-to-get-worse/)
- Collections status: as of May 2026 the New York Fed described collection efforts on defaulted federal loans as suspended with no clear resumption date — [Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/05/federal-student-loan-defaults-return-after-pa); as of 5 Oct 2026 Forbes reported no date had been set — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/adamminsky/2026/10/05/234-billion-in-student-loans-are-now-in-default-and-things-are-about-to-get-worse/). A Stateline story dated 12 Nov 2026 is headlined "Collections are coming for millions of student loans in default" but the page could not be retrieved, so the current status is unconfirmed — [Stateline (headline only)](https://stateline.org/2026/11/12/collections-are-coming-for-millions-of-student-loans-in-default/)

### Inferences
- The task premise of "resumption of collections" is only partly supported by what I could read: delinquency reporting resumed in Q1 2025 and defaults began in Q4 2025, but involuntary collection (garnishment, offsets) appears to have still been paused as of early October 2026, with November 2026 headlines suggesting it is imminent. If garnishment starts, it would hit borrowers whose other debts are already heavily delinquent.
- Getting better or worse: inflow is clearly better (halved year on year); the stock is still rising slowly and a second wave from about 7 million former SAVE borrowers is a known forward risk through 2027.
- The "back to pre-pandemic" level of the 90+ share understates the shock because borrower credit scores had been inflated during the pause and then fell sharply.

## 3. By age and income: New York Fed transition rates; which groups are above 2019 and above 2009?

### Takeaway
By age, the under-30s have the highest transition rates into serious delinquency on both cards (10.1%, Q2 2026) and auto (4.79%, four quarters to Q3 2026), with card rates for the young near 2010 levels and for the over-70s at the highest since Q3 2011. By ZIP-code income, the New York Fed says card delinquency is above pre-pandemic levels in every income quartile and the gap between low and high-income areas has widened, but it gives no numbers in the text, and it expects improvement as the weak 2020 to 2022 card vintages age out.

### Cited Findings
- Credit card transition into serious delinquency by age, Q2 2026 (NY Fed data relayed by a secondary source): 18 to 29 10.1% (+0.4 pp in the quarter); 30 to 39 8.3%; 40 to 49 7.6%; 50 to 59 6.4%; 60 to 69 5.0%; 70+ 6.3% (+0.3 pp). The 18 to 29 rate has more than doubled since Q2 2021 and is "close to levels last seen around 2010"; the 70+ rate is the highest "since the third quarter of 2011"; 50 to 59 highest since Q4 2024 — [24/7 Wall St., 29 Sep 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/29/retirement-nightmare-serious-credit-card-delinquencies-for-seniors-hit-15-year-high/)
- Auto transition into 90+ day delinquency by age, four quarters ending Q3 2026 (NY Fed data relayed by a secondary source): 18 to 29 4.79%; 30 to 39 3.84%; 40 to 49 2.97%; 50 to 59 2.19%; 60 to 69 1.69%; 70+ 2.56%; the under-30 rate declined slightly from a year earlier — [CU Collector, 15 Nov 2026](https://blog.cucollector.com/seriously-delinquent-auto-loan-debt-jumps-15-billion-as-borrower-distress-persists/)
- All-age card flow into serious delinquency: 6.87% in Q3 2026 vs 7.05% in Q3 2025; all debt 2.64% vs 3.03% — [NY Fed press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110). Card flow has declined for three straight quarters; share of card balances seriously delinquent 12.9% in Q3 2026 — [NAHB Eye on Housing](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/11/household-debt-delinquencies-rise-as-mortgage-performance-weakens/)
- By ZIP income (NY Fed, 10 Nov 2026, four equal-population quartiles): card delinquency rose sharply from 2022 to 2024, steepest in lower-income neighborhoods; the gap between higher and lower-income neighborhoods "has expanded"; recent rates are above pre-pandemic levels for all groups. No quartile values are given in the text (chart data only) — [Liberty Street Economics, 10 Nov 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/11/a-look-at-the-wider-divide-in-credit-card-delinquency-by-income/)
- Why (same post): the 2020 to 2022 card vintage was issued at lower scores (median score at issuance 688 vs 699 for 2017 to 2019), with the share of new cards and limits going to the lowest-income quartile up nearly 2 percentage points at the start of the pandemic; that vintage's delinquency rose rapidly and peaked through 2023 into 2024; inflation outpaced wages for low-income brackets and pandemic savings were exhausted. Authors judge macro conditions the main driver, credit easing secondary, and expect elevated delinquency to fade as these vintages shrink, absent a macro shock — [Liberty Street Economics, 10 Nov 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/11/a-look-at-the-wider-divide-in-credit-card-delinquency-by-income/)
- Overall historical position: 5.0% of household debt in some stage of delinquency in Q3 2026 (up 0.3 pp in the quarter) — [NY Fed press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110); share of consumers with a third-party collection 4.9% in Q3 2026 vs more than 14% from 2008 through 2015; foreclosures (61,900 in Q3 2026) at the very low end of the three pre-pandemic years — [Wolf Street, 11 Nov 2026](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/11/11/household-debts-debt-to-income-ratios-delinquencies-foreclosures-collections-bankruptcies-in-q3-2026/)
- Different income lens (wealth): the poorest half of households by wealth held 51.6% of all consumer credit in Q2 2026 (Federal Reserve, via secondary) — [24/7 Wall St., 2 Nov 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/02/auto-delinquencies-are-near-a-10-year-high-and-it-could-get-a-lot-worse/)

### Inferences
- Above 2019: every ZIP-income quartile for cards (NY Fed statement); by implication young borrowers on cards and auto. Above or near 2009 to 2011: under-30 card transitions (near 2010), over-70 card transitions (highest since 2011), auto 90+ share overall. Well below 2009: mortgages, foreclosures, collections, and aggregate flow.
- The age pattern is a barbell: youngest and oldest cohorts are deteriorating at the margin (both rose in Q2 2026) while the aggregate card flow is improving.
- The NY Fed's vintage explanation implies the low-income card problem is partly a one-off underwriting cohort effect that is fading, not a steadily worsening trend.

### Gaps
- Numeric transition rates by ZIP-income quartile, with 2019 and 2009 comparison values: not found (in the NY Fed chart data file, not read).
- Age-group transition rates for Q3 2026 for cards, and 2019 and 2009 comparison values by age for any product: not found.
- A third-party headline says delinquencies are the "highest since 2016" (TheStreet, title only) while Wolf Street says delinquency rates are at or below the three pre-pandemic years; I could not reconcile these because the first page was not read.

## 4. Mortgages: FHA and VA versus conventional, foreclosure starts, and what is behind the rise in serious delinquency flow

### Takeaway
The rise in mortgage delinquency (NY Fed flow into serious delinquency 1.60% in Q3 2026 vs 1.28% a year earlier) is overwhelmingly an FHA story, with VA a distant second and conventional loans barely changed: FHA delinquency was 11.79% in Q2 2026 (MBA) against 2.72% conventional, and FHA loans were 47.2% of all mortgages 60+ days past due in Q3 2026 (TransUnion) while only 18.5% of originations. Levels remain low versus 2008 to 2010, and the NY Fed calls the rate "modest by historical standards."

### Cited Findings
- NY Fed: flow into serious mortgage delinquency 1.60% in Q3 2026 vs 1.28% in Q3 2025; HELOC 1.17% vs 1.27% — [NY Fed press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110). Quarter on quarter 1.6% from 1.5%; serious delinquency rate 1.1% of balances, up 0.1 pp — [NAHB Eye on Housing](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/11/household-debt-delinquencies-rise-as-mortgage-performance-weakens/)
- NY Fed text: mortgage early-delinquency transitions "continued to tick up"; serious transitions worsened but "remain modest by historical standards"; about 62,000 individuals had a new foreclosure notation in Q3 2026, up from Q2; about half of the $257bn rise in mortgage balances reflects re-reporting after a Q2 2026 reporting gap; mortgage origination scores fell 6 points at the 25th percentile and 3 points at the 10th — [NY Fed HHDC 2026Q3](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/pdf/hhdc_2026q3.pdf)
- MBA National Delinquency Survey Q2 2026 (seasonally adjusted, released 13 Aug 2026): total 4.37% (−7 bp q/q, +44 bp y/y); conventional 2.72% (−3 bp q/q, +12 bp y/y); FHA 11.79% (−9 bp q/q, +122 bp y/y); VA 4.89% (−10 bp q/q, +57 bp y/y). Seriously delinquent rate y/y change by type: FHA +227 bp, VA +31 bp, conventional +6 bp. Foreclosure starts 0.2% (−4 bp q/q); foreclosure inventory 0.67% (+3 bp q/q, +19 bp y/y); 90-day bucket 1.43% (+1 bp). A 2.06% seriously delinquent rate (+49 bp y/y) is reported; the article's labelling is ambiguous but this appears to be the all-loan rate — [HousingWire, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/mortgage-delinquency-q2-2026/)
- MBA cause attribution (Marina Walsh, Q2 2026): "Some loans are continuing to move to later stages of delinquency"; labor market weakness, rising delinquencies on student loans, cards and auto, stretched affordability and slower equity accumulation — [HousingWire, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/mortgage-delinquency-q2-2026/)
- One-year-earlier baseline, MBA Q3 2025: total 3.99%; conventional 2.62%; FHA 10.78%; VA 4.50%; seriously delinquent (all loans, NSA) 1.61%; foreclosure starts 0.20%; foreclosure inventory 0.50%. Walsh cited softer labor market, other personal debt, and rising taxes, insurance and fees for FHA borrowers; home price declines in some areas limiting the ability to sell or refinance; and flagged the end of COVID-era FHA loss mitigation options as a future driver — [MBA Newslink, 17 Nov 2025](https://newslink.mba.org/?p=265362)
- TransUnion Q3 2026: FHA loans were 47.2% of mortgages 60+ days past due, the highest concentration in more than 20 years, vs 18.5% of new originations; FHA delinquency 5.60% vs 4.09% a year earlier; borrower-level 60+ day mortgage delinquency 1.64% vs 1.36% (Q3 2025), 1.20% (Q3 2024), 0.95% (Q3 2023); Fannie, Freddie and jumbo performance "remained stable"; TransUnion's Satyan Merchant says FHA is more of a driver than pre-pandemic, when delinquency was spread more evenly (secondary source relaying TransUnion) — [United States Real Estate Investor, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.unitedstatesrealestateinvestor.com/transunion-q3-2026-fha-delinquency-47-percent/)
- TransUnion: Q4 2025 mortgage 60+ delinquency 1.57%, the 16th straight quarter of year-over-year increases — [National Mortgage Professional, 4 May 2026](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/transunion-flags-k-shaped-credit-divide-mortgage-delinquencies-climb-refi-activity-surges)

### Inferences
- What is behind 1.28% to 1.60%: (a) FHA borrowers (lower scores, high LTV, small down payments, 2022 to 2024 purchase vintages with little equity); (b) the end of pandemic-era FHA loss-mitigation options, which lets loans roll to later stages and into foreclosure (inventory +19 bp y/y); (c) escrow shocks from taxes and insurance; (d) cross-default from student loans (20% of student defaulters past due on mortgages) and other consumer debt; (e) flat or falling prices in some markets removing the sell-or-refinance exit.
- Conventional +6 bp y/y in serious delinquency suggests the prime mortgage book is essentially intact; this is contained by loan type for now.
- Versus 2008 to 2010: no source read gave the comparison number, but the NY Fed's "modest by historical standards" and Wolf Street's foreclosure comment place mortgages far below crisis levels.

### Gaps
- MBA National Delinquency Survey for Q3 2026 (residential): not found in search (may not yet be published or indexed as of 17 Nov 2026). Latest read is Q2 2026.
- FHA seriously delinquent rate level (not change) and 2009 to 2010 peak values for FHA/total: not found.
- NY Fed mortgage delinquency by loan type or by age for Q3 2026: not found.

## 5. Lower-income consumers: what consumer companies say; share of spending from the top 10% or 20%

### Takeaway
Company commentary through early November 2026 consistently describes low-income customers as constrained (fuel, prices) and value-seeking, with growth at value retailers coming disproportionately from middle and high-income "trade-in"; McDonald's posted its first US same-store decline since early 2025. But card data complicate the K-shape story: Bank of America shows lower-income card spending and wage growth currently running ahead of higher-income groups in October 2026, and the Minneapolis Fed finds the "top 10% is half of spending" claim rests on one disputed method.

### Cited Findings
Companies
- McDonald's Q3 2026 (reported 4 Nov 2026): US same-store sales −0.2% in July to September, the first decline since early 2025 (analysts expected −0.5%); turned positive in September and October; US fast-food traffic −2.6% in Q3 (Revenue Management Solutions). CEO Chris Kempczinski: "You need to have good entry-level value, particularly with the low-income consumer"; the company has tried for over two years to win back lower-income customers; testing bundles such as a McChicken and small fries for $3.50 at 1,900 restaurants; in August only 60% of franchised restaurants offered the revamped McValue menu — [AP via KPTV, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.kptv.com/2026/11/04/mcdonalds-us-sales-fall-more-customers-struggle-with-high-cost-living/?outputType=amp)
- Dollar General Q2 2026 (reported 27 Aug 2026): same-store sales +3.5%, traffic +2%, customers buying fewer items per trip; CEO Todd Vasos: core customers "continue to be financially constrained" (fuel, inflation), with "strong trade-in across middle- and high-income" consumers — [PYMNTS, 28 Aug 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/economy/2026/dollar-stores-see-high-income-shoppers-hunting-value/)
- Dollar Tree Q2 2026 (reported 27 Aug 2026): comparable sales +3.7%, ticket +3.3%, traffic +0.4%; consumables comps +5.8% vs discretionary +1.6%; CEO Mike Creedon: inflation pressuring budgets "particularly for lower-income consumers"; gains across all income cohorts, strongest among middle and high-income — [PYMNTS, 28 Aug 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/economy/2026/dollar-stores-see-high-income-shoppers-hunting-value/)
- Chipotle Q3 2026 (reported 28 Oct 2026): comparable sales +2.1% (check +1.3%, transactions +0.8%), third consecutive quarter of positive transaction growth; full-year comps guided to low single digits; the release contains no statement on income cohorts — [Chipotle 8-K exhibit, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001058090/000105809026000103/cmg-20261028xex991.htm)
- Cox Automotive (September 2026): "Affordability issues remain – just not for everyone"; but its Consumer Health Index for $50K-plus households "has fallen sharply toward zero over the last four months" while under-$50K households have been climbing off some of the weakest readings in the series; "the gap between the top and bottom of the income ladder is compressing" — [Cox Automotive transcript](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Q3-2026-Cox-Automotive-Sales-Forecast-Update-Transcript.pdf)

Card and wage data by income
- Bank of America Institute, October 2026: card spending per household +5.8% y/y (September +6.2%); ex-gasoline +4.6% (September +5.1%). By income: lower-income +5.9%, top 5% +5.7%, middle and higher "around 5%". After-tax wage growth: lower-income 4.7%, middle just below 4%, higher 3.4%. Spending growth has outpaced after-tax wage growth for all cohorts for five months, most for the top 5%. The top 5% still leads on discretionary spending but the gap to lower-income is the smallest since May 2025. Deposits remain above inflation-adjusted 2019 levels for all income groups; the 2026 tax-refund boost has "run its course". Holiday average ticket is below 2025, suggesting trade-down; about 46% of lower-income survey respondents plan to spend less than last year — [BofA Institute Consumer Checkpoint, 12 Nov 2026](https://institute.bankofamerica.com/content/dam/economic-insights/consumer-checkpoint-november-2026.pdf)

Share of spending by income
- Moody's: top 10% of households account for more than 45% of spending, the bottom 60% for 23%; top-10% spending grew 62% from Q3 2020 to Q3 2025. Dallas Fed variant: top 20% averaged 57% of spending over 2020 to 2025. But the Consumer Expenditure Survey (2024) puts the top 10% at 23% and the top 30% at more than half; an experimental CE-PCE hybrid puts the top 10% at 25.7% in 2023 (25.0% in 2020, 26.3% in 2000). Moody's figure is derived from income minus estimated saving, not measured spending. Conclusion: "the available data do not align to tell a clear, K-shaped story"; BofA data were "arguably more 'E' than 'K'" — [Minneapolis Fed, 20 Mar 2026](https://minneapolisfed.org/article/2026/have-us-consumers-gone-k-shaped-a-review-of-the-data)
- Wealth concentration: top 10% of households hold 88% of equities, bottom 50% less than 1% (period not stated) — [Cox Automotive transcript](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Q3-2026-Cox-Automotive-Sales-Forecast-Update-Transcript.pdf)

### Inferences
- Nominal lower-income spending growth of 5.9% with gas prices up about 41% and ex-gas growth a point lower likely reflects necessity inflation rather than strength; this is consistent with "fewer items per trip" at Dollar General and consumables-led comps at Dollar Tree.
- Middle and high-income trade-in to dollar stores and BofA's lower holiday ticket sizes are behavioural signs of caution moving up the income scale, even while those households' credit performance is fine.
- The "top 10% = about half of spending" statistic should be attributed to Moody's with the Minneapolis Fed's caveat; direct survey measures give roughly half that share.

## 6. Other distress markers: hardship withdrawals, bankruptcies, BNPL, rent and evictions, utilities, paycheck-to-paycheck

### Takeaway
Secondary markers show broad but slow-moving strain rather than acute crisis: 401(k) hardship withdrawals hit a record 6% of Vanguard participants in 2025 (2% in 2020), BNPL use for groceries and BNPL late payments have each risen sharply for two years, utility arrears are growing fastest in near-prime tiers, while bankruptcy growth has slowed to 2% year on year in October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Vanguard hardship withdrawals: 6% of participants in 2025, a record, up from 5% in 2024 and 2% in 2020 — [WTOP, June 2026](https://wtop.com/news/2026/06/401k-hardship-withdrawals-are-at-record-highs-what-they-really-cost-you/). Withdrawals "skew towards those making $100,000 or less"; average Vanguard 401(k) balance rose 13% in 2025 to $167,970; main reasons are avoiding foreclosure or eviction and medical expenses — [HR Brew, March 2026](https://www.hr-brew.com/stories/2026/03/13/record-number-401-k-hardship-withdrawals)
- Fidelity: average 401(k) balance fell 4% in Q1 2026 from the prior quarter — [WTOP, June 2026](https://wtop.com/news/2026/06/401k-hardship-withdrawals-are-at-record-highs-what-they-really-cost-you/)
- Bankruptcies (Epiq AACER, October 2026 vs October 2025): total 54,028, +2% (53,033); individual 51,266, +2%; individual Chapter 7 31,594, +2%; individual Chapter 13 19,591, +3%; commercial 2,762, −4%; Subchapter V elections 280, +24%. Month on month total +3% from 52,310 in September. Epiq's Michael Hunter: "We expect filing volumes to keep rising into 2027" — [ABI / Epiq, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.abi.org/node/1004659)
- NY Fed: about 135,000 consumers had a new bankruptcy notation in Q3 2026, a slight decrease from Q2; third-party collections 4.9% of consumers — [NY Fed HHDC 2026Q3](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/pdf/hhdc_2026q3.pdf)
- BNPL (LendingTree surveys, March 2026): 29% of BNPL users have used it for groceries (25% in 2025, 14% in 2024); Gen Z 38%, boomers 16%, users with children 34%, users earning $100,000 or more 33%. 47% of users paid late in the past year (41% in 2025, 34% in 2024) — [LendingTree BNPL tracker](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/buy-now-pay-later-loan-statistics/). Note: a 16 Nov 2026 TV report quotes 25% for groceries and 41% late, which match LendingTree's 2025 figures, not 2026 — [KAUZ/InvestigateTV, 16 Nov 2026](https://newschannel6now.com/2026/11/16/more-people-are-using-buy-now-pay-later-basic-expenses)
- Utility debt (The Century Foundation, data to March 2026): about 1 in 20 households (roughly 14 million Americans) have utility debt in or about to be sent to collections; average overdue balance $817; NEADA: 1 in 6 households behind on utility bills, owing $25bn; deep subprime 18% vs super-prime under 1%; since December 2024 average overdue balances grew 13.6% for subprime and 12.6% for near-prime vs 6.9% for deep subprime; over four years deep-subprime bills rose 52% vs 35% for super-prime — [The Century Foundation](https://tcf.org/content/report/power-failure-rising-energy-debt-is-climbing-into-the-middle-class/)
- Evictions (dated, January 2026 article): Eviction Lab, tracking 30+ cities, found filings "have returned to near-historic averages and, in some places, surpassed them"; Nashville filings about 46% above the 2023 to 24 average over the 12 months through late 2025; Minnesota over 23,000 cases by late November 2025 vs roughly 15,000 a year pre-pandemic — [BiggerPockets, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.biggerpockets.com/blog/eviction-filings-spiking-2026-what-landlords-need-to-know)
- Sentiment and labor context: University of Michigan sentiment 48.1 (down from 51.7); unemployment 4.2% in September 2026 (4.1% in August) (via secondary) — [24/7 Wall St., 2 Nov 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/02/auto-delinquencies-are-near-a-10-year-high-and-it-could-get-a-lot-worse/)

### Inferences
- Hardship withdrawals tripling since 2020 partly reflects easier rules (self-certification, SECURE 2.0) as well as stress; it should not be read purely as distress.
- Bankruptcy growth decelerating to +2% y/y in October 2026 (Chapter 13 slightly faster than Chapter 7) is not consistent with an accelerating consumer credit event, though Epiq expects continued increases.
- BNPL for groceries at 33% among users earning $100,000+ and utility arrears growing fastest in near-prime are two of the few direct data points for stress in the middle.

## 7. Is stress moving from subprime into prime, or from lower into middle-income households, in 2026?

### Takeaway
There is real but limited evidence of upward creep in 2026, concentrated in auto and in early-stage delinquency: prime and super-prime auto delinquencies are rising while subprime improves, the near-prime middle is shrinking and carrying the largest debt-to-income increases, and higher-income sentiment and behaviour (trade-in to dollar stores, Cox's $50K-plus index) have weakened. It is not yet showing up in aggregate flows into serious delinquency, which improved year on year for cards, student loans and total debt, nor in conventional mortgages; so the best description is "contained in loan type and credit tier, with early signs of spread at the margins."

### Cited Findings
Evidence of spreading
- Experian Q2 2026: 60-day delinquencies on prime and super-prime auto loans rose to 0.7% while subprime improved; Fitch prime auto 60-day index 0.49% in July 2026 vs 0.42% a year earlier; the article says prime delinquencies are rising while subprime is improving (Experian, VantageScore) — [Yahoo Finance / The Auto Wire, 21 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/repo-watch-good-credit-got-220000409.html)
- TransUnion (2019 to 2025): super-prime share reached 40.7% in 2025 (+380 bp since 2019, about 15 million more consumers); subprime share has begun rising again; prime and near-prime shares declined. Non-mortgage debt-to-income rose 176 bp for near-prime and 143 bp for subprime vs 29 bp for super-prime; TransUnion calls the market "moving in two directions at once" — [National Mortgage Professional, 4 May 2026](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/transunion-flags-k-shaped-credit-divide-mortgage-delinquencies-climb-refi-activity-surges)
- Utility arrears growing faster for near-prime (+12.6%) and subprime (+13.6%) than deep subprime (+6.9%) since December 2024; authors say rising costs are reaching families who previously kept up — [The Century Foundation](https://tcf.org/content/report/power-failure-rising-energy-debt-is-climbing-into-the-middle-class/)
- Cox: Consumer Health Index for $50K-plus households fell sharply toward zero over the four months to September 2026, compressing the gap with under-$50K households — [Cox Automotive transcript](https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Q3-2026-Cox-Automotive-Sales-Forecast-Update-Transcript.pdf)
- Middle and high-income trade-in to Dollar General and Dollar Tree (Q2 2026) — [PYMNTS, 28 Aug 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/economy/2026/dollar-stores-see-high-income-shoppers-hunting-value/); BNPL-for-groceries at 33% among BNPL users earning $100,000+ (March 2026) — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/buy-now-pay-later-loan-statistics/)
- NY Fed: card delinquency above pre-pandemic in all ZIP-income quartiles, including the highest — [Liberty Street Economics, 10 Nov 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/11/a-look-at-the-wider-divide-in-credit-card-delinquency-by-income/); early-stage transitions ticked up for mortgages and auto in Q3 2026; share of debt in any delinquency rose 0.3 pp to 5.0% — [NY Fed HHDC 2026Q3](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/pdf/hhdc_2026q3.pdf)
- VantageScore headlines: January 2026 "early-stage credit stress broadens across borrowers"; September 2026 "consumer credit remains stable", with a trade summary titled "Consumer Credit Stable as Early Delinquencies Rise" (titles only; pages not read) — [VantageScore Jan 2026](https://vantagescore.com/resources/knowledge-center/press_releases/vantagescore-creditgauge-january-2026-mortgage-delinquencies-rise-as-early-stage-credit-stress-broadens-across-borrowers); [VantageScore Sep 2026](https://vantagescore.com/resources/knowledge-center/press_releases/vantagescore-creditgauge-september-2026-overall-balances-hold-steady-as-consumer-credit-remains-stable)

Evidence of containment
- Flows into serious delinquency fell year on year for cards (6.87% vs 7.05%), student loans (7.72% vs 14.26%), other (5.13% vs 5.35%), HELOC (1.17% vs 1.27%) and all debt (2.64% vs 3.03%); only mortgages (1.60% vs 1.28%) and auto (3.02% vs 2.99%) rose, Q3 2026 vs Q3 2025 — [NY Fed press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110)
- Mortgage deterioration is FHA-concentrated: conventional serious delinquency +6 bp y/y vs FHA +227 bp (MBA Q2 2026) — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/mortgage-delinquency-q2-2026/); GSE and jumbo performance "remained stable" (TransUnion Q3 2026) — [United States Real Estate Investor](https://www.unitedstatesrealestateinvestor.com/transunion-q3-2026-fha-delinquency-47-percent/)
- Prime auto delinquency (0.49%, July 2026) remains about half its Great Recession level (~0.9%) — [Yahoo Finance / The Auto Wire](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/repo-watch-good-credit-got-220000409.html)
- NY Fed expects card delinquency to keep improving as 2020 to 2022 vintages age out, absent a macro shock — [Liberty Street Economics, 10 Nov 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/11/a-look-at-the-wider-divide-in-credit-card-delinquency-by-income/)
- Lower-income wage growth (4.7%) and card spending growth (5.9%) are currently ahead of higher-income groups (October 2026), and deposits are above real 2019 levels for all income groups — [BofA Institute, 12 Nov 2026](https://institute.bankofamerica.com/content/dam/economic-insights/consumer-checkpoint-november-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The migration that is visible is "near-prime and prime auto borrowers with $1,000 payments and negative equity" and "FHA/VA homeowners," not a general prime or middle-income credit deterioration.
- Middle-income stress shows first in behaviour and non-credit arrears (trade-down, BNPL for essentials, utilities), which typically lead credit-bureau delinquency; the rise in early-stage mortgage and auto transitions in Q3 2026 is the indicator to watch.
- Two-way risk: spending is outrunning after-tax wages for all cohorts (five months per BofA), fuel is up about 41% year on year, and unemployment is edging up (4.2%); versus fading bad card vintages and falling student-loan inflows.
- Relative to 2008 to 2010 the current episode is narrower (unsecured, auto, student, FHA) and lacks the conventional mortgage and foreclosure channel; relative to 2019 it is worse in auto, cards for young and low-income borrowers, FHA, and non-credit hardship markers.
