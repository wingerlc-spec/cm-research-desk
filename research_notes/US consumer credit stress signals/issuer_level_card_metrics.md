Conventions: NCO = net charge-off rate (annualized, % of average loans); DQ = delinquency rate (% of period-end loans). Every number below was read in the cited source during this session. "Computed" marks simple arithmetic on cited numbers. "Not found" means I searched and could not retrieve it. Several secondary pages (beancount.io, finsee.ai, Earnings Whispers, Quartr, rallies.ai) are AI-assisted summaries; I used them only where a primary filing was unreachable and flag each such use.

## 1. Latest card NCO and 30+ DQ by issuer: Q3 2026 and October 2026, versus Q3 2025 and 2019

### Takeaway
Every one of the eight issuers reported a lower card loss rate in Q3 2026 than in Q3 2025, with declines of roughly 10 to 100 bps; delinquencies are flat to modestly lower (0 to 47 bps). Where a 2019 comparison could be sourced (Synchrony, Capital One, Fed aggregates), losses are now at or below 2019 levels while 30+ delinquency is about equal to 2019.

## 2. Who is improving, flat or worsening; is there a prime versus subprime / private-label split?

### Takeaway
No issuer in the set is getting worse on losses year over year; the largest improvements are at the highest-loss, lowest-FICO books (Bread -99 bps, Capital One -65 bps, Citi private label -48 bps, Synchrony -37 bps), so the level gap between prime and private-label persists but is narrowing rather than widening. The split shows up in levels, in smaller-bank data and in underwriting appetite, not in the direction of the big issuers' trends.

### Cited Findings
- Improving on both NCO and DQ year over year: Bread (NCO -99 bps, DQ -47 bps), Capital One (NCO -65 bps, DQ -15 bps), Citi PLCC (NCL -48 bps, 90+ -16 bps), Citi GPCC (NCL -34 bps, 90+ -6 bps), Bank of America (NCO -24 bps, 30+ -9 bps), Amex (write-off -10 bps, 30+ -10 bps) — computed from the filings cited in section 1.
- Improving on losses, flat on delinquency: Synchrony (NCO -37 bps; 30+ 4.34% vs 4.39%; management called 30+ and 90+ "in line with the prior year") — [Synchrony Q3 2026 call transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15781703289185280); Wells Fargo (30+ 2.69% vs 2.68%, 90+ unchanged at 1.34%, card losses "lower") — [Wells Fargo 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000072971/000007297126000327/wfc-20260930.htm)
- Sequential (Q2 to Q3 2026) upticks in delinquency are seasonal: Capital One 30+ 3.39% to 3.74%, Bank of America 2.23% to 2.32% — [Earnings Whispers COF](https://www.earningswhispers.com/article/COF/2610200363); [BofA supplemental](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000070858/000007085826000481/bac-09302026ex993.htm). One summary notes Capital One's bank-wide delinquencies rose 27 bps from June versus 16 bps in the same period a year earlier — [finsee.ai](https://finsee.ai/earnings/cof/2026/q3/en/) (AI-assisted summary; not verified against the filing)
- Level gap in Q3 2026: Bread 6.40% and Synchrony 4.79% versus JPMorgan 2.91%, Bank of America 3.22% and Amex 1.8% — sources in section 1.
- Citi's private-label book is shrinking (average loans $28B vs $30B) while general purpose grows ($149B vs $137B, helped by the American Airlines co-brand portfolio onboarded in April 2026); about 86% of Citi card balances are FICO 660 or higher; CFO Gonzalo Luchetti said "some of the marginal segments probably below the FICO band" may feel pressure from cumulative rate increases — [Citi Q3 2026 transcript](https://www.citigroup.com/rcs/citigpa/storage/public/Earnings/Q32026/3q26-citigroup-earnings-transcript.pdf); [Citi 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000831001/000083100126000056/c-20260930.htm)
- Smaller banks (outside the top 100), which skew subprime, show charge-offs of 7.80% in Q2 2026, down from 8.64% a year earlier but above every 2019 quarter (6.60% to 7.11%) — Federal Reserve CORCCOBS (local CSV; [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CORCCOBS))
- Capital One said in October 2025 that "we are seeing subprime credit performance track similarly to prime credit" — [Capital One Q3 2025 call](https://equibles.com/stocks/cof/documents/9e3c1104-20fd-427c-bc64-f90b26038e67) (a year-old statement; no Q3 2026 equivalent found)

### Inferences
- The visible divergence is by lender size and tier rather than among the eight large issuers: large-bank charge-offs are back inside the 2019 range, small-bank charge-offs are not.
- The bigger basis-point improvements at Bread, Synchrony and Citi private label partly reflect a higher starting point and deliberate mix shift (fewer non-prime accounts, more co-brand), see section 5.
- Amex's small improvement is flattered by a model-driven reserve release and a changed reporting base (card balances), so its trend is best read as flat at a very low level.

## 3. What management said on Q3 2026 calls

### Takeaway
The tone was uniformly constructive: losses running at or below guidance, 2026 loss guidance cut or pointed to the low end at Synchrony, Bread, Citi and JPMorgan, and payment rates up. Reserves moved in mixed directions (releases at Capital One and Amex, small builds at Synchrony, Bread, Citi and JPMorgan's CCB), and the only explicit 2027 comments say improvement slows (Bread) or losses rise as new programs season (Synchrony).

### Inferences
- Reserve direction no longer tracks loss direction: Synchrony, Bread and Citi all built reserves while reporting lower losses, citing growth or macro outlook, which reads as caution about 2027 rather than about current performance.
- Synchrony's own decomposition (56 of 60 bps of payment-rate improvement from mix) is the clearest management admission that better metrics are compositional, see section 5.
- Citi's falling non-interest revenue is the mirror image of good private-label credit: partners capture part of the loss improvement through revenue sharing, as also happens at Synchrony through retailer share arrangements.

## 4. Are issuers tightening or loosening? What does the SLOOS say?

### Takeaway
Large issuers are cautiously reopening for prime and co-brand customers (Synchrony "started to open up", Capital One raising marketing, Bank of America new accounts up 9%) while staying tight at the bottom of the credit spectrum. The October 2026 SLOOS shows standards basically unchanged in aggregate, tighter credit limits and below-threshold lending, banks more willing to approve prime and super-prime and less willing to approve subprime, and stronger card demand.

### Cited Findings
- October 2026 SLOOS (covers Q3 2026; responses due October 5, 2026): banks left credit card standards and most terms "basically unchanged"; exceptions were tighter credit limits on card accounts and tighter extent of lending to borrowers below credit-score thresholds; demand for credit card loans strengthened — [Federal Reserve SLOOS, October 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/sloos/sloos-202610.htm)
- SLOOS special questions (past year): banks more likely to approve super-prime and prime card applicants and less likely to approve subprime applicants, the subprime result driven by small banks; near-prime basically unchanged; the most-cited reason for stronger demand was customers' spending needs in response to price changes for goods and services — [Federal Reserve SLOOS, October 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/sloos/sloos-202610.htm)
- About 10% of banks (net) reported stronger card demand, the highest since 2022 — [KPMG on the Q3 2026 SLOOS, November 2, 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/q3-2026-fed-sloos.html)
- Philadelphia Fed large bank data, Q2 2026: card dollar originations up 1.5% YoY (smallest gain in a five-year run); new accounts up nearly 4%; average new credit line fell to $6,066 from $6,216; share of new accounts with scores below 660 rose to 18.5% from 16.1% (peak was 27% more than a decade ago); median new line for scores below 660 is $500 to $600 versus $7,000 for 720+; total card balances up 3.4% YoY, revolving balances up 1.5%; full-payment share 37%, matching the Q2 2021 peak — [Philadelphia Fed Q2 2026 large bank report](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/2026-q2-large-bank)
- Synchrony: Doubles said "We started to open up a bit earlier in the year just given how good credit was looking"; average active accounts 68.3M, flat YoY; purchase volume up 8% — [Synchrony call transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15781703289185280); [Synchrony 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001601712/000160171226000043/syf-20260930.htm)
- Capital One: marketing up 14% and rising in 2027, aimed at Discover products, premium cards and national banking; Discover balances down about 3% under a temporary growth limit — [Zacks call summary](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/3011873/cof-q3-earnings-call-highlights-integration-and-spending-plans)
- Bank of America: new card accounts 1,016K in Q3 2026 vs 929K in Q3 2025 (computed +9%); average balances up about 5% (computed) — [BofA supplemental](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000070858/000007085826000481/bac-09302026ex993.htm)
- Wells Fargo: new card accounts 661K vs 707K (computed -7%) while card loans grew to $54.5B from $51.6B — [Wells Fargo 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000072971/000007297126000327/wfc-20260930.htm)
- Bread: average loans up 5.7% YoY in Q3 2026 and 6.9% in October 2026, after -1.3% in October 2025 — [Bread September](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001101215/000110121526000088/september2026creditstatsex.htm) and [October 2026 credit stats](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001101215/000110121526000102/october2026creditstatsex99.htm)
- KBRA (March 2026, data through Q4 2025): new commitments to scores below 660 were 3.8% of the total, among the lowest in the series; new accounts to scores below 660 were 16.4% vs 25.2% in Q4 2021; card balances grew a net $37.1B in 2025 vs $50.4B in 2024 — [KBRA Financial Intelligence](https://www.kbra.com/analytics/products/kfi/insights/credit-card-boom-slows-as-borrower-quality-remains-resilient)

### Inferences
- 2026 is a "selective reopening": account counts to sub-660 borrowers are rising from a low base (16.1% to 18.5% of new accounts) but with very small lines, so dollar exposure to subprime stays minimal.
- Stronger card demand attributed to higher prices, combined with tighter limits, is a potential stress signal: demand is need-driven while supply is being rationed at the low end.
- Bread and Synchrony returning to mid-single-digit loan growth means the "denominator" tailwind to their loss rates turns positive again in late 2026, and new vintages will season into 2027 losses.

## 5. How much of the improvement is underwriting, denominator and seasoning rather than healthier consumers?

### Takeaway
No source quantifies the split for losses, but the evidence that a large share is compositional is strong: issuers themselves attribute improvement to past credit actions and "higher-quality" new accounts, Synchrony attributes more than 90% of its payment-rate gain to mix, and industry data show sub-660 lending near series lows through 2025. A precise decomposition was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Synchrony: of the roughly 60 bps YoY rise in payment rate, "over 90% of that or 56 basis points is really mix related" (35 bps from new portfolios, 8 bps from lower promotional balances); non-prime share of the book is "a couple of 100 basis points lower" and may rise back to a "more normal" level; management cites "previous credit actions" (2023 to 2025) now seasoning — [Synchrony call transcript](https://www.webull.com/news/15781703289185280)
- Bread: improvement attributed to "sophisticated underwriting, and further maturation of higher-quality new account acquisitions" — [Bread Q3 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001101215/000110121526000087/exh9913q26bfhepr_final.htm)
- Capital One (October 2025): Discover "cut origination programs for 2023 to 2025"; 2024 originations "tracking at or below pre-pandemic levels for both Capital One and Discover"; of the 98 bps YoY improvement in the Q3 2025 Domestic Card NCO, 19 bps came from adding Discover — [Capital One Q3 2025 call](https://equibles.com/stocks/cof/documents/9e3c1104-20fd-427c-bc64-f90b26038e67)
- Capital One 2026: recoveries $1.68B, up 14% YoY, support the net charge-off rate — [finsee.ai](https://finsee.ai/earnings/cof/2026/q3/en/) (AI-assisted summary)
- Citi: the shift toward general purpose cards (average loans +9%, private label declining) is cited by the CFO as a credit positive — [Citi Q3 2026 transcript](https://www.citigroup.com/rcs/citigpa/storage/public/Earnings/Q32026/3q26-citigroup-earnings-transcript.pdf)
- Amex: reported as "card balances" from Q1 2026, adding pay-in-full receivables to the base; the Q3 reserve release came from model enhancements — [American Express 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/4962/000000496226000409/axp-20260930.htm)
- Industry: share of new card accounts below 660 fell from 25.2% (Q4 2021) to 16.4% (Q4 2025); minimum-payment share 10.7% in Q4 2025, just below the 11.1% record of Q4 2024; full-payment share 36.1% (Q4 2025) and 37% (Q2 2026) — [KBRA](https://www.kbra.com/analytics/products/kfi/insights/credit-card-boom-slows-as-borrower-quality-remains-resilient); [Philadelphia Fed Q2 2026](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/2026-q2-large-bank)
- Philadelphia Fed says card delinquency and charge-off rates have declined for more than a year and are moving closer to pre-pandemic levels, while revolving balance growth slowed to 1.5% YoY — [Philadelphia Fed Q2 2026](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/2026-q2-large-bank)

### Inferences
- Denominator effects now cut both ways. In 2025 shrinking or flat books (Bread average loans -1.3% in October 2025, Discover balances contracting) inflated loss rates; in 2026 renewed growth (Bread +6.9%, Synchrony +3.2%, Capital One ex-Discover about +6%) mechanically lowers them, because new balances carry few losses for 12 to 24 months.
- A bimodal payment picture (full-payer share at a record-matching 37% while minimum-payer share sits just below its record) is what tighter underwriting plus a strained existing lower tier would produce: the portfolio got better at the top by selection, not because the weakest borrowers recovered.
- Portfolio transfers muddy comparisons: Discover (lower loss) entering Capital One in May 2025, the Lowe's commercial co-brand portfolio ($0.7B) entering Synchrony in April 2026, American Airlines co-brand balances moving to Citi in April 2026, and Synchrony's Walmart/OnePay program ramping with losses still ahead.
- With issuers now reopening and sub-660 share of new accounts rising, the seasoning tailwind should fade in 2027; Bread and Synchrony both signal this.
